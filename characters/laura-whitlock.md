# Laura Whitlock

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Name | Laura Whitlock |
| Age | 38 |
| Gender | Female |
| Role | Jonas's wife; the personal stake of the story |
| Job | General practitioner (family doctor) at a surgery in south London, back at work part-time after maternity leave |

## Profile

Laura is practical, quick and unsentimental in the way doctors become. She deals every day with people who are frightened and need a straight answer, and she expects the same from her husband. She has no interest in finance and no patience for jargon, which makes her the reader's stand-in: when Jonas has to explain something to her, he has to explain it plainly.

She and Jonas have been married for six years. Their daughter Elsie is one.

## Wants

A husband who is present. A safe, ordinary life for Elsie.

## Fears and flaws

- She suspects the nightly writing is a place Jonas goes to avoid her, and she has stopped asking about it.
- She is slow to accept that the danger is real and physical, because nothing in her life has prepared her for that.
- Once she does accept it, she will not be sent away quietly.

## Relationships

- **Jonas:** love, worn thin by his silence.
- **Elsie:** their daughter, aged one.
- **Her mother:** lives in Norfolk; the place Jonas wants to send them.

## Voice

Direct questions. Medical dryness. She notices what people's bodies are doing while they talk.

## Arc in act I

From hosting a birthday dinner for a distracted husband to leaving London with a child and a bag, knowing only that he has been keeping something from her for four years.

## Still to decide

- Whether she stays out of the action after act I or becomes an active player.
- Whether her patients give a street-level view of the panic: pensions, savings, queues at banks.
