# Caroline Strand

## Profile

Strand is a former barrister who rose by being better prepared than everyone else in the room. She is disciplined, controlled, and has a reputation for competence that is her party's main asset. A general election is five weeks away and the polls are close.

Three years ago a memo from a risk manager at a clearing house crossed her department's desk, warning of exactly what is now happening. It was noted and filed. If that becomes public in the middle of a campaign, she is finished, and so is the government. The record proves the warning was given, when, and to whom.

She is not corrupt. She believes that confidence is the only thing holding the system up, that admitting doubt would cause the collapse she is trying to prevent, and that the country is better off with her in charge. Each of those beliefs is defensible. Together they lead her to treat the truth as the threat.

## Wants

To get through five weeks. To stop the panic by denying there is one. The record buried.

## Fears and flaws

- She confuses her own survival with the national interest.
- She has never been wrong in public and does not know how to be.
- She uses the state's powers for a political end and tells herself it is financial stability.

## Relationships

- **The prime minister:** needs her and would drop her in an hour.
- **Graham Pryce:** a name on a briefing note until this week.
- **Sebastian Crane:** a donor she now keeps at arm's length.
- **Khalid Al-Mansouri:** his fund is one of the largest foreign holders of British government debt.

## Voice

Precise, lawyerly, never an unfinished sentence. On television, warm. In private, cold.

## Arc in act I

From "the system is sound" as a holding line to using it as a weapon against the one man who can show it is false.
