## Profile

Al-Mansouri runs one of the largest pools of money in the world on behalf of a state. He was educated in London and Boston, worked at a Western investment bank in his twenties, and understands both worlds well enough to be trusted fully by neither. He is courteous, exact, and thinks in decades.

His fund holds vast positions in British and European bonds, with collateral lodged at Walbrook. When a tanker is sunk in the Strait of Hormuz and Western politicians start talking about freezing the assets of whoever is to blame, his duty is clear: get his country's money somewhere it cannot be seized. That rational act of self-protection is what turns a market wobble into a global panic.

He is neither villain nor friend. He is a man with one client and an obligation to it.

## Wants

To protect his country's wealth from being frozen. To know who really sank the tanker, because his government is being blamed.

## Fears and flaws

- If he moves too slowly, the assets are frozen. If he moves too fast, he destroys their value himself.
- His position at home depends on a ruling family whose factions disagree about the crisis.
- He holds a grudge against Sebastian Crane and it may cloud his judgement.

## Relationships

- **Sebastian Crane:** a former investor in Greywether; he withdrew after concluding Crane could not be trusted.
- **Caroline Strand:** counterpart and adversary across a negotiating table.
- **Jonas:** they have never met. Jonas knows the fund only as the largest line in his collateral reports.

## Voice

Formal, patient, fond of understatement. Says less than he knows.

## Arc in act I

Gives the order to withdraw his fund's collateral, knowing what it will do.
