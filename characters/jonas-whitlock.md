# Jonas Whitlock

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Name | Jonas Whitlock |
| Age | 40 (his birthday falls on the second day of the story) |
| Gender | Male |
| Role | Narrator and protagonist |
| Job | Head of collateral risk, Walbrook Clearing House, London |

## Profile

Jonas is the man whose job is to know what everyone has promised to everyone else, and whether the promises are backed by anything. He is careful, dry, and more comfortable with a spreadsheet than a meeting. People underestimate him because he does not raise his voice.

For almost four years he has kept a private record: one handwritten entry a day, numbered, in a run of plain notebooks. It began as a way to think. It became a log of warning signs that the same bonds were being pledged as collateral many times over, so that a sharp enough shock would force everyone to sell at once. Chapter 1 is entry 1399.

## History

- **25 to 30:** a trader at a bank. Good money, late nights, nothing to show for it. He calls these "good, but pointless years".
- **30 to 35:** moved from trading into risk, then between employers. Some growth, "still floating around".
- **35:** joined Walbrook.
- **36:** noticed the first anomaly in the collateral data and began the record.
- **37:** wrote a formal memo estimating that the system had about three years before it broke. It was filed and ignored. This is the "three years" of chapter 1.
- **39:** his daughter Elsie was born. The record stopped being an intellectual exercise and became a duty. "Life has different meaning."

## Wants

To be proved wrong. Failing that, to be listened to before it is too late.

## Fears and flaws

- He has been right and silent for too long, and part of him knows the record was a way of being right without taking a risk.
- He trusts numbers over people and misreads the people.
- He would protect Laura and Elsie at the cost of everything else, and his enemies work that out quickly.

## Relationships

- **Laura:** his wife. She knows he writes every night; she does not know what.
- **Priya Raman:** the only colleague he trusts with the real numbers.
- **Graham Pryce:** a boss he respects and is disappointed by.
- **Caroline Strand:** her Treasury received his memo three years ago.

## Voice

First person, in the record. Short sentences, fragments, numbers. He counts things: years, days, entries, exposures.

## Arc in act I

From a private observer who writes things down to a man who has taken the record and run.
