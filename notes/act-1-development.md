# Act I development suggestions

Suggested by an agent on 2026-10-03 and accepted by the author on 2026-10-07. Turned into [outline/act-1.md](../outline/act-1.md). Kept here as the reasoning behind the outline.

## Proposed shape

| Chapters | Beat | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Entry `#1399`, turning 40 | Sets the voice and the record. Kept short; chapter 2 carries the hook |
| 2 | Someone learns the record exists | Puts a threat on the page before the panic starts |
| 2–4 | The narrator's ordinary world | The job, the 40th birthday, and one relationship that will be put at risk |
| 4–6 | First shock: the geopolitical trigger | The inciting event. Officials call it contained; the narrator recognises it from the record |
| 6–9 | The warning is ignored | National politics enters. The narrator tries proper channels and is shut out. First physical danger |
| 9–12 | Second shock and a countdown | The panic spreads. A weekend before markets reopen gives a natural ticking clock |
| 13–14 | Point of no return | The narrator makes a choice they cannot undo, and the panic goes global |

## Suggestions, by problem

- **The record had no subject.** Make it the narrator's private log of warning signs that the system would break. This resolves "When 37 it was three years": at 37 the narrator judged it would take three years, which lands on 40. Daily entries put the start around age 36.
- **A panic has no villain.** Act I needs a human opponent: someone who profits from the panic, or someone who needs the narrator silent. Resolved in the outline as two forces: Sebastian Crane, who profits, and Caroline Strand, who needs the record buried.
- **Politics was abstract.** Give each force one face: a finance minister facing an election (Caroline Strand) and one counterpart abroad (Khalid Al-Mansouri).
- **Action must arrive early.** One physical set piece in the first 60 pages (chapter 8: the search of the house), and a colleague who disappears (chapter 11).
- **Finance could slow the opening.** Teach the mechanism that will later break through one small, ordinary problem in the narrator's working day (chapter 4).
- **Drama needs an anchor.** The birthday dinner shows who is there, who is missing, and what the narrator stands to lose (chapter 3).
- **Entry numbers should work, not decorate.** Numbers run forward from 1399 as a day count, giving the book a clock. Occasional old entries serve as short flashbacks.
