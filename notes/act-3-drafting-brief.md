## Revisions to outline/act-3.md (decided 2026-10-10)

The outline was written before acts I–II were drafted. These adjustments make it fit the book as it stands; where they conflict with the outline, follow these.

- **Calendar.** Wednesday #1421 is one long day (ch. 43–50). Thursday #1422 (ch. 51), Saturday #1424 (ch. 52), polling day Thursday #1436 (ch. 53), then #1500 eleven weeks after the broadcast, New Year's Eve (ch. 54–55). Jonas numbers days, not pages.
- **43, the broadcast (Jonas, 6am).** Live from the cupboard studio two floors below Calloway's desk, as planned in ch. 42: what Walbrook is, why it cannot pay what it owes tomorrow, the twelve members by name in order, Greywether's trade dates against the sinking, Crane by name, the envelope held up, Faisal's office *not* named, a buyer with money in London at seven, the missing signature. The paper's lawyers have tried to stop it; Calloway has made the editor choose. Eleven minutes. He is arrested in the lobby at about 6.40, as Calloway predicted, with the envelope; the original swap confirmation is bagged. Laura and Elsie were taken out through the loading bay at 4.30 and are not in the building.
- **44, the signature (Strand, 7am).** She watches it. The prime minister rings before it ends. She signs the one-page indemnity and has Carrow write to the police a second time, withdrawing the Treasury's concern about Whitlock: the official writes, the police decide. She also writes her resignation, to be dated the day after polling day. Her reasoning is a lawyer's: the alternative is indefensible.
- **45, assembly (Jonas, 7.30am–9am).** Not in Walbrook's risk room: Jonas is released from the police station at about 8.15 after the Governor and the Treasury's second letter ask that he be available to assist the Bank (bail with a new condition; Kessler's envelope stays in evidence, the paper has a photograph). He is taken by car to the fund's St James's dealing room. Present: Nasser, the fund's sterling-book head, its legal man, the Bank by video (the Governor's deputy), Priya by phone from the hospital side room with Pryce's papers beside her, Calloway outside. The Bank and the exchanges have delayed the open to ten; Walbrook's deadline has moved from nine to noon. The transaction is built line by line from the map: which collateral, which order, which price. Jonas is the only person who knows the whole order, and says so.
- **46, front-running (Crane, 8am).** He has watched the broadcast with his lawyers on the phone. He understands the plan before anyone explains it: they will buy what he has shorted. Two moves: get ahead of them in the market, and stop the man who holds the order in his head. His two former surveillance men are his; Kessler is gone. He sends them first for Jonas's whereabouts, then, for leverage, for the child. He says the word to one of them himself. Show him as intelligent, patient and, for the first time, hurried.
- **47, the thirteenth (Jonas, 8.40am).** Priya finds the hole by phone: Greywether has been quietly building a position against a thirteenth member that was sound four years ago and so is not in the record. If the fund buys the twelve in order, Crane's attack on the thirteenth breaks the chain at the end. The fix is to buy out of order; the Bank's people say it cannot be done in the time. Jonas says the order is in his head and he will give it live.
- **48, Cannon Street (Jonas, then Laura, 9.15am).** Walbrook isolated its external links on the Bank's advice at six, so the only way to give the order to Walbrook's people in time is a person. Jonas must physically get from St James's to Walbrook's risk room by ten. Crane's men are waiting in the City. A pursuit through the morning crowd: Cannon Street, the alleys behind Bank, a station concourse; he is caught briefly and gets free because he is not worth killing in front of three hundred commuters. He arrives with blood on the borrowed shirt. Second section, close third, Laura: the grey car at the end of the road where she and Elsie are waiting (decide where: Tom and Aisha's in Herne Hill, which Crane's men found from the car that followed her in ch. 35). She takes out Kessler's card and rings the number. Kessler comes. She does not hurt anyone; she tells her former colleagues, in the doorway, exactly what will happen to them and who will be told. The grey car leaves. Nobody is hurt. This pays off the child threat from ch. 39–40 and Laura's card from ch. 35.
- **49, the open (Jonas, 10am).** The transaction. The first buy goes through. Then the second. Jonas calls the order, out of sequence, from memory, with Priya correcting him twice on the line. Prices stop falling, then rise. Greywether's shorts, which paid out by the minute, begin to lose by the second. At 10.42 Greywether receives a margin call it cannot meet. The thirteenth is bought last, as Priya's fix requires. Walbrook's fund is topped up from the proceeds; the clearing house meets its noon deadline.
- **50, without screens (Crane, 11am).** Told by telephone his fund is finished. He still does not understand how a man who wrote in notebooks beat him. Pell does not take his call. The police, who want to talk to him about the dates of certain trades, do; they arrive at the Mayfair building with a warrant. The steel cabinet is opened. The nine notebooks, the Treasury's stamped copy of the memo and the unsigned statement are in it.
- **51, home (Al-Mansouri, Thursday #1422).** The fund has made money by saving the market it was blamed for breaking. Instead of the noon flight, he went on Wednesday's to be arrested or thanked, and is thanked, because the council's decree was read to an empty chair and then withdrawn. Faisal is not seen in public again. He sends Jonas one formal, short message.
- **52, statements (Jonas, Saturday #1424).** Jonas gives his statement; the police no longer wish to arrest him. Priya is offered a job by the Bank and asks for the weekend, which is his line and they both know it. Pryce has retired to Suffolk; the order against him is dropped. Kessler meets Jonas on a bench; she has closed her firm. She asks what he will do with the notebooks, returned as evidence and then to him. He says he will finish them. Haldane and Pell: one paragraph, no more.
- **53, polling day (Strand, #1436).** Her party loses narrowly. She resigns as drafted. In her last interview she is asked whether she regrets ignoring the memo and says, for once, yes. She means it, which surprises her more than anyone.
- **54, the last page (Laura, about New Year).** Laura watches Jonas at the kitchen table in Herne Hill, writing. Elsie is walking. He has been offered a great deal of money to write a book and has refused. He has taken a job at the Bank, in the room where the transaction was built. He closes the tenth notebook, with pages still blank in it, and does not open another.
- **55, #1500 (Jonas).** The final entry. Short, mirroring chapter 1 in length and voice. He does not count the years this time. He writes what he has learned in one line, then writes about Elsie's first word, which is not the one he thought it would be. He signs the entry, which he has never done, and stops. Eight hundred to twelve hundred words.

## Facts every act III chapter must respect

- **The transaction** (ch. 36, 37, 41): the fund buys exactly what each of the twelve is forced to sell, in map order, through its three clearing banks; the Bank lends against each purchase the same morning via those banks under a one-page Treasury indemnity; the Bank asks the exchanges to open late and Walbrook to move its nine o'clock deadline. Al-Mansouri's money is in the three banks' accounts by seven, his father's branch's two London accounts first; his signed instruction names "the fund's adviser, Mr J. Whitlock, or on his behalf by the chief of staff". The council's decree is read at 8am London and ends his authority; he left the noon flight uncancelled.
- **The map**: twelve four-letter codes and an order; Jonas's rebuilt page has Walbrook's September figures in the right-hand column and one corner brown with Priya's blood; the original is in Crane's steel cabinet. Priya's red book is with Crane's men. Nobody has the fingerprints except Jonas and the nine books. The mechanism is still inferred, never seen; Jonas can be wrong and says so, and in act III it is the part of him that makes him say "I think" while he gives an order.
- **Places and people on Tuesday night** (end of ch. 42): Jonas, Laura and Elsie at Calloway's second-floor flat in Kentish Town; Calloway waking the editor; Priya in hospital in a side room under a false name Jonas never writes, plaster to the elbow, eleven stitches, Pryce's sixteen box files beside her; Pryce at home, resigned; Al-Mansouri and Nasser in the Park Lane suite, sound on; Kessler's firm closed, SIM out; Crane in Mayfair with the notebooks and the original page in his cabinet; Strand with her initials beside the three-years sentence and "prudence" in Sunday's minutes; the Governor lending to the second member; Haldane's further statement unissued; the second member's parent suspended; the fourth named in a broker's note.
