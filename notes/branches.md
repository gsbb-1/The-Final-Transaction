# Branches

Story directions that were considered, kept so nothing is lost. How branching works: [WORKFLOW.md](../WORKFLOW.md). Decisions are recorded in the decision log in [PLAN.md](../PLAN.md).

## From the end of chapter 1 (2026-10-03)

Decision: **C, "The record"**, chosen by the author.

Revised the same day: the fraud at the narrator's firm in branch C was replaced by a global market panic with geopolitical risk and national politics as the plot device. The record and its numbered entries stay. The text of branch C below is as originally proposed.

### A: The exit plan (not chosen)

- **What happens:** At 37 the narrator, a banker or trader, gave themselves three years to get out by 40. `#1399` is the number of the last deal. Chapter 2 opens on that deal, and it goes wrong.
- **Consequences:** The story runs forward in a straight line from the 40th birthday. The "final transaction" is in play from chapter 2.
- **What it gives:** The strongest ticking clock and the fastest start.
- **What it costs:** "One last job" is a familiar shape. The antagonist has to be invented from scratch.
- **Fit:** Thriller and action strong; drama weaker unless the reason for leaving is personal.
- **Note:** Could combine with C: the record could end at the deadline the narrator set for getting out.

### B: The sentence (not chosen)

- **What happens:** The three years were a prison term, or three years under investigation, for a financial crime at 37. `#1399` is an inmate or case number. The narrator comes out near 40 and goes after whoever let them take the fall.
- **Consequences:** The crime sits in the past and is revealed in pieces. Old colleagues become suspects and targets.
- **What it gives:** A built-in antagonist, a motive, and a mystery about what really happened.
- **What it costs:** The narrator starts outside the industry and needs a credible route back in. Revenge plots need a twist to feel fresh.
- **Fit:** Suspense and drama strong; action depends on how dangerous the enemies are.

### C: The record (chosen)

- **What happens:** `#1399` is an entry number. Since discovering a fraud at their firm around 37, the narrator has kept a secret record, and entry 1399 is the last one before they act on it. Chapter 2 shows someone else learning the record exists.
- **Consequences:** Every chapter can open with an entry number, which gives the book a structure and lets it move between past entries and the present. The record becomes an object everyone wants.
- **What it gives:** The finance plot arrives naturally, the reader knows about a danger the narrator does not, and chapter 1's fragment style is explained as a journal voice.
- **What it costs:** The numbering must stay consistent for the whole book. If entries are daily, 1,399 days is about three years and ten months, so the record began around age 36, which must be squared with "when 37" in chapter 1.
- **Fit:** Suspense and thriller strong; action has to come from the hunt for the record.

### D: The diagnosis (not chosen)

- **What happens:** At 37 the narrator was given three years to live. Turning 40 is the deadline, and with nothing left to lose they set out to make one last transaction: to take down, or rob, the institution they served.
- **Consequences:** The ending is constrained from the start; the question becomes what the narrator leaves behind and for whom.
- **What it gives:** The most personal stakes and a literal meaning for the title.
- **What it costs:** Invites comparison with well-known stories; risks melodrama; a late cure or misdiagnosis would feel like a cheat.
- **Fit:** Drama strongest; thriller and action depend on the plan.
