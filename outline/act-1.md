# Act I outline

Chapters 1 to 14, about pages 1 to 100. Written by an agent at the author's request, from the development suggestions in [notes/act-1-development.md](../notes/act-1-development.md) and the cast in [characters/](../characters/README.md). Chapter 1 is the author's text; chapters 2 to 14 are a proposal and none of them is drafted.

## Working assumptions

These were chosen by the agent to make an outline possible. Each is the author's to confirm or change.

| Assumption | Choice |
| --- | --- |
| Place | London. Real cities and markets; all people, firms and funds invented |
| Time | The present; the year is not fixed |
| Narrator | Jonas Whitlock, head of collateral risk at Walbrook Clearing House |
| The record | Handwritten notebooks, one numbered entry per day, begun when Jonas was 36. A log of warning signs that the same bonds are pledged as collateral many times over |
| Point of view | Jonas's chapters are entries from the record, in first person. Chapters he cannot witness are in third person, following one other character each |
| Chapter headings | Every chapter opens with the entry number for its day. Old entries appear as short flashbacks |
| Trigger | A tanker is sunk in the Strait of Hormuz; who did it is disputed |
| National politics | A general election is five weeks away; Chancellor Caroline Strand must show the system is sound |

## Chapters

### 1. Life lesson — #1399, Tuesday — Jonas (written)

The author's text. Jonas, on the eve of turning 40, counts his life in five-year blocks.

- **Purpose:** sets the voice and the record.
- **Threads opened:** what `#1399` is; the "three years" at 37; what changed by 39; the "life lesson".

### 2. The wrong inbox — #1400, Wednesday — Graham Pryce

On his birthday morning Jonas photographs last night's entry to keep a copy and, tired, sends it to his work address instead of his own. Walbrook's compliance filter flags the words in it and routes the message to the chief risk officer. Graham Pryce reads one page of a diary that is plainly entry 1,399 of something, and understands that his head of collateral risk has been documenting for years a danger Graham signed off as contained. He does not speak to Jonas. He calls the chief executive.

- **Purpose:** the decided beat: someone else learns the record exists. Puts a threat on the page before the panic starts.
- **Pays off:** what `#1399` is.
- **Opens:** who Graham's call reaches.

### 3. Forty — #1400, Wednesday — Jonas

Birthday dinner at home in south London. Laura has cooked; Elsie, aged one, will not sleep. Two friends ask Jonas what he actually does and he gives the short answer. Laura watches him leave the table to write. Afterwards she asks, not for the first time, what is in the notebooks, and he says "work". The entry he writes that night is about Elsie.

- **Purpose:** the personal stake. Shows what Jonas has to lose and the distance in the marriage.
- **Pays off (partly):** what changed by 39. The reader sees Elsie; why that changed the meaning of the record is held for later.

### 4. Twice — #1401, Thursday — Jonas

An ordinary working day. A pension fund's collateral fails a routine check: the bonds it has posted to Walbrook appear to be pledged somewhere else as well. Priya Raman finds it, is told by the fund that it is a timing error, and does not believe it. Jonas walks her through why it matters, in plain terms, and the reader learns the mechanism: when prices fall, everyone must post more collateral at once, and if the same bonds back several promises, there is not enough to go round. Jonas has seen this a hundred times. It is in the notebooks. He tells Priya to write it up and expects nothing to come of it. Graham is oddly formal with him all day.

- **Purpose:** teaches the mechanism that will break, through one small problem. Establishes Priya.
- **Opens:** Priya's suspicion that Jonas knows more than he reports.

### 5. Hormuz — #1401, Thursday night — Khalid Al-Mansouri

In the Gulf, Khalid Al-Mansouri is woken with the news that a loaded tanker has been sunk in the Strait of Hormuz. Within the hour the strait is closed to shipping and his own government is being named on Western news channels as the likely culprit. He knows it is not true, or believes he does. He asks for a list of every asset his fund holds in London and where the collateral sits. The largest line is Walbrook.

- **Purpose:** the inciting event. Brings in the geopolitical side through one person.
- **Opens:** who sank the tanker; whether Western governments will freeze the fund's assets.

### 6. Contained — #1402, Friday — Jonas

Markets open sharply down. Oil jumps, inflation fears follow, and British government bond prices fall hard. Walbrook's margin calls for the morning are the largest Jonas has seen, and they are met, just. On the trading floor screens Caroline Strand tells a television interviewer that the financial system is sound and the situation contained. Jonas goes back through the record that evening and finds the pattern he wrote down long ago. The chapter includes an old entry, #212, in which he first described it.

- **Purpose:** first shock. The narrator recognises it from the record. Introduces Strand and the election.
- **Uses:** the first flashback entry.

### 7. Not yet — #1402, Friday — Jonas

Jonas takes his numbers to Graham and asks for a formal escalation to the regulator. Graham says "not yet". Jonas goes over his head and files it himself. By late afternoon his system access has been narrowed, and Graham, unable to meet his eye, tells him the chief executive wants to see "any personal notes relating to Walbrook's business". Jonas understands that they know about the record, and how.

- **Purpose:** the warning is ignored through proper channels. Jonas learns he is exposed.
- **Opens:** how far up the knowledge has travelled.

### 8. The study — #1402, Friday evening — Sebastian Crane, then Jonas

In Mayfair, Sebastian Crane hears from his friend on the Walbrook board that a risk manager has kept a four-year diary of where the bodies are buried. Crane's fund is already positioned for a collapse; the record would tell him where to push. He calls Dana Kessler.

Jonas notices he is being followed on the way to the lock-up (as drafted: he goes from Walbrook to Lewisham before home) and loses the tail on the Underground at London Bridge, half convinced he is imagining it. At the house the study has been searched, neatly. Nothing is missing because the notebooks are not kept there. Laura and Elsie were out. He does not tell Laura.

- **Purpose:** introduces the antagonist and the physical threat. The first action beat, inside the first 60 pages.
- **Opens:** where the notebooks are.

### 9. Withdrawal — #1403, Saturday — Khalid Al-Mansouri

Overnight, politicians in London and Washington have begun to talk publicly about freezing the assets of the state they blame for the sinking. Al-Mansouri makes his decision. He instructs his fund to give notice that it will withdraw its collateral from Walbrook and sell its British bonds when markets reopen. He takes a call from Sebastian Crane, who offers to buy the position privately at a steep discount, and refuses it. He knows what his order will do. He gives it anyway.

- **Purpose:** the second shock, and the moment the crisis stops being local.
- **Opens:** the old grudge between Al-Mansouri and Crane.

### 10. The memo — #1403, Saturday — Jonas

Jonas is summoned to an emergency meeting at the Treasury: the Chancellor, the central bank, the regulator, Walbrook's chief executive and Graham. He is there because he is the one person who can explain the collateral chain, and he does. Then an official slides a document across the table: his own memo from three years ago, when he was 37, estimating that the system had three years. They have had it all along. Strand does not want his analysis. She wants his signature on a statement that Walbrook's collateral is fully sound, to be released before markets open, and she wants his "personal notes" handed to the Treasury for safekeeping. He asks for the night to think.

- **Purpose:** national politics at full strength. Jonas meets Strand.
- **Pays off:** the "three years" from chapter 1.
- **Opens:** the countdown to the market open.

### 11. Monday's number — #1404, Sunday — Jonas

Priya has worked through the night on her own. She calls Jonas with Monday's number: once the Gulf fund withdraws, the margin calls Walbrook must issue at the open will be more than its members can pay. At least one large member fails, and Walbrook itself is then in question. She has also worked out that Jonas has known the shape of this for years, and she is furious. She says she is going to send the figures to the regulator and to a journalist, and hangs up. An hour later she is not answering. Jonas goes to her flat. The door is unlocked and she is not there.

- **Purpose:** raises the stakes to their act I maximum and removes Jonas's only ally.
- **Opens:** what has happened to Priya.

### 12. The offer — #1404, Sunday afternoon — Jonas

Outside Priya's building a woman is waiting for him. Dana Kessler introduces herself, is courteous, and makes an offer on behalf of a client she does not name: a very large sum for the notebooks, today. She mentions, without emphasis, the route Laura takes to the surgery and the name of Elsie's nursery. She says she would much prefer he took the money. He has until the evening.

- **Purpose:** the antagonist's hand shown directly. The family becomes a target.
- **Opens:** whether Kessler is behind Priya's disappearance.

### 13. The lock-up — #1404, Sunday night — Jonas, then Laura

Jonas tells Laura enough to frighten her and not enough to satisfy her, and sends her and Elsie to her mother's in Norfolk. A short section from Laura's point of view, driving north in the dark with a sleeping child, working out how much she has not been told. Jonas goes to the lock-up garage he inherited from his father, where the notebooks are kept. Kessler's people have followed him. He gets out with the notebooks, on foot and then by river path and night bus, and it is not graceful. Across the world, the first Asian markets are opening.

- **Purpose:** the main action sequence of act I. The record becomes something Jonas carries.
- **Pays off:** where the notebooks are.

### 14. The open — #1405, Monday — Jonas

Jonas does not sign. At seven, Walbrook issues the largest margin call in its history. On breakfast television Caroline Strand says the system is sound and refers to "irresponsible claims from a disaffected individual". By eight a major clearing member has failed to pay, the pound is in freefall, and trading halts are spreading from London to New York. Jonas's building pass no longer works. Walbrook has reported the theft of confidential material and the police want to speak to him. Sebastian Crane's positions are paying out by the minute, and he still does not have the map. Jonas has four years of notebooks in a rucksack, a wife who is not answering, a colleague who has vanished, and nowhere to go. He writes entry 1405 on a bench.

- **Purpose:** the point of no return. The panic goes global; Jonas cannot go back to his desk.
- **Leaves open for act II:** Priya; who sank the tanker; what Jonas will do with the record; whether Al-Mansouri is an ally; what "the final transaction" is.

## To verify in research

The agent is not certain of these details; check them before drafting.

- How a clearing house would see that bonds are pledged more than once, and whether Jonas's vantage point is realistic for it.
- How quickly a sovereign fund could withdraw collateral and sell, and what notice is required.
- The timing of margin calls, and of Asian market opening hours in London time.
- What powers a Chancellor and the Treasury have over a clearing house and its staff in a crisis.
- How compliance email filters work, and whether the slip in chapter 2 is plausible.
- How an oil shock feeds through to government bond prices and the pound.
