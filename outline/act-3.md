## Chapters

### 43. Six o'clock — #1421, Wednesday — Jonas

Live from the newspaper's studio at 6am, before any market opens. Jonas, in a borrowed shirt, explains in plain words what Walbrook is, why it may not be able to pay what it owes tomorrow, and how the collapse was foreseen. Then he names the twelve members, in order. Then he names Greywether, shows the dates of its trades against the date of the sinking, and says that whoever planned the sinking had a partner in Mayfair. He does not name Faisal, because he does not need to. He is on air for eleven minutes.

- **Purpose:** the private record made public; the act of a man who has stopped writing things down.

### 44. The signature — #1421, 7am — Caroline Strand

The prime minister rings before the broadcast is over. Her choice: sign the indemnity and stand behind a Gulf-funded rescue, or let Walbrook fail on her watch and lose the election anyway. She signs. She also drafts her resignation, to be dated the day after polling day. It is the first decision in the book she makes against her own interest, and she makes it as a lawyer: because the alternative is indefensible.

- **Purpose:** Strand's turn; the state behind the transaction.

### 45. Assembly — #1421, 7.30am — Jonas

Walbrook's risk room. The Bank's people, Al-Mansouri's traders by video, Pryce, Jonas, and Priya in a sling. Haldane, grey, doing as he is told. The transaction is built line by line from the map: which collateral, in which order, at what price. The Bank and the exchanges have delayed the open to ten to give them the time. Jonas is the only person who knows the whole map and the order; he reads it out from memory while Priya checks it against Pryce's data.

- **Purpose:** the mechanics as suspense; competence under a clock.

### 46. Front-running — #1421, 8am — Sebastian Crane

Crane has watched the broadcast. He understands the plan before anyone explains it: they will buy what he has shorted. He has two moves: get ahead of them in the market, and stop the one man who holds the order in his head. He does the first himself. For the second, with Kessler gone, he uses the people Kessler would not.

- **Purpose:** the antagonist's last throw; the physical threat renewed.

### 47. The thirteenth — #1421, 8.40am — Jonas

Priya finds a hole. Greywether has been quietly building a position in a thirteenth member, one not in the record because it was sound four years ago. If the transaction buys the twelve in order, Crane's attack on the thirteenth breaks the chain at the end. The fix is to buy out of order, which the Bank's people say is impossible in the time. Jonas says the order is in his head and he will give it to them live.

- **Purpose:** the reversal inside the climax; the map was not enough, the man is needed.

### 48. Cannon Street — #1421, 9.15am — Jonas

Jonas has to physically get from the newspaper's building to Walbrook, because the secure link has been cut and the order must be given in the room. Crane's men are waiting. A pursuit through the City in the morning crowd: Cannon Street, the alleys behind Bank, a station concourse. He is caught, briefly, and gets free because he is not worth killing in front of three hundred commuters. He arrives with blood on the borrowed shirt.

- **Purpose:** the action climax, in daylight, in the place where money lives.

### 49. The open — #1421, 10am — Jonas

The transaction. The first buy goes through. Then the second. Jonas calls the order, out of sequence, from memory, with Priya correcting him twice. Prices stop falling. Then they rise. Greywether's positions, which paid out by the minute a week ago, begin to lose by the second. At 10.42 Greywether receives a margin call it cannot meet.

- **Purpose:** the final transaction, executed.

### 50. Without screens — #1421, 11am — Sebastian Crane

Crane in his room with no screens, being told by telephone that his fund is finished. He still does not understand how a man who wrote things in notebooks beat him. Sir Anthony Pell does not take his call. The police, who want to speak to him about the dates of certain trades, do.

- **Purpose:** the antagonist's fall; he never understands it.

### 51. Home — #1422, Thursday — Khalid Al-Mansouri

The fund has made a great deal of money by saving the market it was blamed for breaking. Al-Mansouri flies home to be either arrested or thanked, and is thanked. Faisal is not seen in public again. Al-Mansouri sends Jonas one message, formal and short.

- **Purpose:** the geopolitical thread closed; the cost of being right in his world.

### 53. Polling day — #1437, Thursday, two weeks later — Caroline Strand

Strand's party loses narrowly. She resigns as drafted. In her last interview she is asked whether she regrets ignoring the memo and says, for once, yes. She means it, which surprises her more than anyone.

- **Purpose:** national politics closed; a politician tells the truth once.

### 52. Statements — #1424, Saturday — Jonas

Jonas gives his statement to the police, who no longer want to arrest him and are not sure what to do with him. Priya is offered a job by the Bank and asks for the weekend to think, which is his line and they both know it. Pryce has retired to Suffolk. Kessler meets Jonas on a bench and tells him she has closed her firm. She asks what he is going to do with the notebooks, which have been returned as evidence. He says he is going to finish them.

- **Purpose:** the small threads tied; the cast released.

### 54. The last page — #1500, about three months later — Laura

Laura watches Jonas at the kitchen table in Herne Hill, writing. Elsie is walking now. He has been offered a great deal of money to write a book and has refused. He has taken a job at the Bank, in the room where the transaction was built. He closes the notebook, the tenth, with pages still blank in it, and does not open another.

- **Purpose:** the family thread closed from the outside; the record ended.

### 55. #1500 — Jonas

The final entry. Short. He does not count the years this time. He writes what he has learned, in one line, and then writes about Elsie's first word. He signs the entry, which he has never done, and stops.

- **Purpose:** the end of the record is the end of the book. Mirrors chapter 1 in length and voice.
