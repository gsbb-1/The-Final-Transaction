# Act II outline

Chapters 15 to 42, about pages 100 to 300. Written by an agent on 2026-10-09 at the author's request. Everything here is a proposal; the author decides. Act I is in [act-1.md](act-1.md); the ending is in [act-3.md](act-3.md); facts are in [continuity.md](continuity.md).

## Decisions this outline makes

Open questions from act I that had to be answered to plan the rest of the book. Each is the author's to change; changing one changes the chapters marked.

## First half: chapters 15 to 28, to the midpoint

### 15. Cash — #1405, Monday night — Jonas

Kessler's 4.30 deadline passes while he is still reading; he lets it. A hotel near King's Cross that takes cash. The evening news has two new things: Walbrook has obtained an interim injunction and an order for delivery-up of "documents containing confidential information", heard by a judge by telephone on Sunday night, and the word "theft" is in every headline although no lawyer has said it. Rolling news: the member that failed is named, the pound is at a level nobody alive has traded, the Chancellor is "monitoring". He rings Laura from a payphone; she answers, is safe at Margaret's, and is angrier than he has ever heard her. He reads the first notebook from the beginning and realises it is a map, if you know how to read it: the twelve members whose collateral is pledged furthest beyond what they hold.

- **Purpose:** breath after the act I climax; establishes the map as the object of the book.
- **Opens:** the twelve names.

### 16. Monitoring — #1406, Tuesday — Caroline Strand

Cabinet Office briefing room. The prime minister, who needs her and would drop her within the hour. The Bank wants to buy bonds to steady the market; Strand blocks it: a bailout four weeks before polling day. Simon Carrow reports that the Walbrook analyst, Raman, was held overnight Sunday to Monday on a Computer Misuse complaint (the member report pulled under her login on Friday after Whitlock's access was cut) and released Monday morning under investigation; the risk manager is still loose; Walbrook's civil order is served on his home address. Strand does not ask who wrote to the police. Carrow did, on Sunday. She says the word "theft" twice so that it will be in the minutes, though she knows it is not the charge.

- **Purpose:** the state becomes the hunter, with a politician's deniability.
- **Pays off:** what happened to Priya, for the reader. Jonas still does not know.

### 17. Off the record — #1406, Tuesday — Jonas

Jonas contacts Rhys Calloway, the journalist Priya named, through the paper's switchboard, and meets him in a café in Clerkenwell. Calloway has Priya's figures but no source he can name and no way to verify them. Jonas shows him one notebook, one page: the memo's date and the three-year estimate. Calloway wants everything. Jonas gives him nothing else yet. On the way out he sees the same woman twice.

- **Purpose:** the press as a route and a risk.
- **Opens:** Kessler has found him again.

### 18. Billions — #1406, Tuesday — Sebastian Crane

Greywether's returns for the day. Investors call to congratulate him and he lets them. Sir Anthony Pell, at lunch, tells him the Treasury are hunting the risk manager themselves. Kessler reports: subject located, notebooks not recovered, the subject has a wife and child in Norfolk. Crane says "Then you know where to apply pressure." Kessler says she does not work that way with children. Crane says he had not realised she had a way.

- **Purpose:** the antagonist's hunger and the first crack between Crane and Kessler.
- **Opens:** Kessler's rule, tested.

### 19. Cousins — #1407, Wednesday — Khalid Al-Mansouri

At home, the fund's withdrawal has been praised in public and questioned in private. A cousin, Faisal, who runs a private family office, is suddenly influential and suddenly rich. Al-Mansouri asks his own trading desk to look at who was positioned against their country's assets before the sinking. One name recurs on the other side of Faisal's trades: Greywether.

- **Purpose:** the geopolitical plot gets a face and a motive.
- **Opens:** who really sank the tanker.

### 20. Released — #1408, Thursday — Jonas

Priya surfaces through Calloway. She was held about twenty hours, released Monday morning under investigation, phone and laptop kept, told not to contact anyone at Walbrook, and has spent three days at her parents' with no phone and no way to reach a man whose own phone is off. The police theory is the report she pulled under her login on Friday for him; the request came from the Treasury; nobody mentioned any hedge fund. Jonas has to revise everything he thought: the state, not Kessler's client, took Priya. Priya has her numbers on paper, in a notebook of her own, because she learned something from him after all. She has not forgiven him.

- **Purpose:** ally restored, enemy count doubled.
- **Pays off:** what happened to Priya, for Jonas.

### 21. The map — #1408, Thursday night — Jonas

In Calloway's flat, Jonas and Priya turn four years of entries into one page: twelve members, the order in which they fail, and what each one is holding. Priya overlays public filings on Greywether's known positions. Crane's trades line up with the record's weak points almost exactly. Then she finds the date: Greywether's biggest positions were put on nine days before the sinking.

- **Purpose:** the map exists; the sinking was anticipated.
- **Opens:** how Crane knew.

### 22. A visitor — #1409, Friday — Laura

Norfolk. A grey car at the end of the lane for the second day. A woman knocks, introduces herself as Dana Kessler, and is courteous. She does not threaten. She says that Jonas has something that does not belong to him and that there are people less patient than her client. She leaves a card. Laura, who deals with frightened people for a living, recognises that she has just been frightened by a professional. She rings Jonas and, when his daily 9am call comes through on Margaret's landline (as drafted: he has no phone of his own that rings Norfolk), says: come here, now, or I am going to the police myself.

- **Purpose:** the family under pressure; Laura stops being passive.
- **Opens:** whether Kessler will cross her own line.

### 23. Norfolk — #1409, Friday night — Jonas

He gets to Norfolk by hired car, train and a lift, shaking a police car at Cambridge. At Margaret's kitchen table he tells Laura everything, from the first anomaly at 36 to the Treasury meeting. It takes two hours. She asks one question: "Did you ever think of telling me?" He has no good answer. Elsie wakes. He holds her. He writes the entry at 3am.

- **Purpose:** the marriage scene; drama at full weight.
- **Partly pays off:** what changed at 39.

### 24. Bailout — #1410, Saturday — Caroline Strand

The Bank's governor tells Strand privately that without intervention a second member fails within days and Walbrook itself is in question. Strand asks what a Walbrook failure means. The governor tells her: every trade it stands behind, frozen. Strand asks for options that do not involve the word "bailout". There are none. She asks for the weekend.

- **Purpose:** the scale of what is coming; Strand's choice framed.
- **Opens:** whether she will act in time.

### 25. Warned — #1411, Sunday — Jonas

Calloway publishes: "Treasury was warned three years ago". The memo's date, its estimate, and the fact that its author is now wanted by the police. Strand denies seeing it, which is true and does not matter. Jonas is named by the paper by lunchtime. He is now famous and hunted at once. Laura watches him become a headline.

- **Purpose:** the story goes public; Jonas loses anonymity.
- **Opens:** who else will come for him now.

### 26. Terms — #1412, Monday — Dana Kessler

Kessler reports to Crane in person. Crane tells her the price has gone up and so has the urgency: a Gulf fund is asking questions about Greywether's trades. He wants the notebooks by the weekend "by whatever route works". She asks whether that includes the child. He does not say no. She leaves without agreeing to anything, and begins, quietly, her own due diligence on her client.

- **Purpose:** Kessler's turn begins.
- **Opens:** what Kessler will find about Crane.

### 27. The largest line — #1412, Monday — Jonas

Jonas goes to the one person with more to lose than he has: the Gulf fund's London office. He gets as far as a lobby and a card. Then a call back, within the hour, from Al-Mansouri himself, who has read Calloway's story and wants to know what else is in the notebooks. A meeting is set for the next evening, at a hotel.

- **Purpose:** the alliance is offered.
- **Opens:** the meeting.

### 28. Midpoint: the hotel — #1413, Tuesday — Jonas, then Dana Kessler

Jonas meets Al-Mansouri in a suite at a Park Lane hotel. Al-Mansouri shows him Faisal's trades: the sinking was arranged by a faction at home, and Greywether took the other side knowing the date. It was never an accident. Al-Mansouri wants the map; Jonas wants a buyer who can stop the selling. They see the same transaction at the same moment. They agree to meet again with the Bank.

Downstairs, Kessler's team takes the rucksack from the cloakroom, politely, with a forged ticket. Jonas comes down to find it gone. Kessler, watching from across the lobby, has the notebooks and, for the first time, doubts about who she is giving them to.

- **Purpose:** the midpoint reversal. The enemy is unified (Crane and the faction), the strongest possible ally is gained, and the record is lost in the same hour.
- **Pays off:** who sank the tanker.
- **Opens:** Crane has the map.

## Second half: chapters 29 to 42, to all is lost

### 29. Reading — #1414, Wednesday — Sebastian Crane

Crane reads the notebooks in his room with no screens. He has the twelve names and the order. He also reads four years of a man's life, and finds it contemptible: so much knowledge, so little done. He plans the final squeeze: concentrated attacks on the three weakest members to break Walbrook's default fund and the clearing house itself. Date: the following Tuesday.

- **Purpose:** the antagonist at full power; the clock for act II's end.

### 30. In his head — #1414, Wednesday — Jonas

The worst entry he has written, on a borrowed pad. Priya finds him. Her copy of the one-page map (ch. 23) is the twelve codes and the order, nothing else: the holdings, the dates and the four years of evidence behind each name went with the notebooks, and a page of codes proves nothing to a central bank. She says: you wrote it; you have read it a hundred times; it is in your head. They start rebuilding the map from memory and from her paper figures. It is slow. They need Walbrook's internal data to be sure.

- **Purpose:** the low after the midpoint; the way back is work.

### 31. The minutes — #1415, Thursday — Caroline Strand

Calloway's second piece: the Treasury asked the police to detain the analyst. Strand's denials are now weaker than the facts. The prime minister gives her until Monday to "stabilise the situation". Alone, she reads the memo for the first time, properly. It is three pages, and it is right.

- **Purpose:** the first crack in Strand.

### 32. I filed it — #1415, Thursday — Graham Pryce

Pryce resigns from Walbrook. He takes nothing with him except what he already had: the risk committee papers he has kept at home for a decade out of habit. He meets Jonas and Priya and gives them the member data. "I filed it," he says. "I'm sorry." It is the only apology in the book and it is not enough.

- **Purpose:** Pryce's redemption; the map can be rebuilt.

### 33. Recall — #1416, Friday — Khalid Al-Mansouri

The faction moves. Al-Mansouri is recalled home "for consultations". He has perhaps a week before he is replaced by someone who will sell everything and let London burn. He tells his London office to prepare for a transaction he does not yet have authority to make.

- **Purpose:** the ally's clock.

### 34. Calloway's office — #1416, Friday night — Jonas

Kessler's team, now acting on Crane's instruction with Kessler herself absent, comes for the rebuilt map at the newspaper's offices. A fight on a stairwell. Priya is hurt, a broken wrist and worse, and taken to hospital under a false name. The map survives because it is on paper in Jonas's coat.

- **Purpose:** the main action sequence of act II; the cost of the fight.

### 35. The press outside — #1417, Saturday — Laura

Photographers at the end of the lane. Margaret frightened. Laura decides she is done being somewhere safe. She packs Elsie and drives to London to find her husband, against his explicit instruction, because she has worked out that nowhere is safe and she would rather be where he is.

- **Purpose:** Laura's choice; the family converges on the danger.

### 36. The transaction — #1418, Sunday — Jonas

Jonas, Priya from a hospital bed, Pryce and Al-Mansouri's London team design the final transaction: one morning, at the open, the Gulf fund buys the weakest members' collateral through Walbrook in the order the map dictates, through an indemnified Bank of England operation: the Bank lends against the collateral the fund buys, the Treasury indemnifies the Bank, and the exchanges and the Bank delay the Tuesday open to give the room time. Done right, the forced selling stops in an hour and Greywether's short positions are squeezed to death. Done wrong, or late, it fails and the Gulf fund fails with it. It needs Strand's signature.

- **Purpose:** the plan the title names; the stakes made exact.

### 37. No — #1418, Sunday — Caroline Strand

Jonas and Al-Mansouri put the plan to Strand in person. She says no. A state publicly blamed for the sinking buying British institutions, indemnified by the taxpayer, two and a half weeks before an election, on the word of a man wanted by the police. She says it is not politics, it is prudence. It is politics.

- **Purpose:** the door closes.

### 38. Tuesday — #1420, Tuesday — Jonas

Crane's attack. The three weakest members hit at the open. By ten, a second member has failed. By noon, Walbrook's default fund is exhausted and Richard Haldane is on television saying the clearing house may not be able to meet its obligations tomorrow; by evening the Bank confirms it is "considering all options including resolution". If Walbrook fails, every trade it stands behind freezes, in London and everywhere London touches. The map was right about everything, including the order.

- **Purpose:** the all-is-lost moment, at the scale the book promised.

### 39. The rule — #1420, Tuesday — Dana Kessler

Kessler's due diligence has reached Faisal's family office and the dates of Greywether's trades. Crane calls and tells her to bring him the child. She says no. He says he will find someone who will. She hangs up, and takes what she has found to the one person it will hurt Crane most to have it: Jonas.

- **Purpose:** Kessler turns. The evidence that Crane knew.

### 40. Entry 1420 — #1420, Tuesday night — Jonas

Laura arrives with Elsie at the flat where he is hiding. He is finished: no notebooks, no clearing house, no signature. He tells her the record was the problem: he spent four years being right in private because it was safer than being wrong in public. Laura says, "Then be wrong in public." He writes the entry to Elsie. It is the life lesson, stated once, plainly, and then not again.

- **Purpose:** the lowest point and the turn, in one scene.
- **Pays off:** the "life lesson"; what changed at 39.

### 41. Without authority — #1420, Tuesday night — Khalid Al-Mansouri

Al-Mansouri, recalled and about to be replaced, decides to act without his government's approval, using the fund's London assets and his own family's money. If it works, he will be forgiven. If it fails, he will not go home.

- **Purpose:** the ally commits everything.

### 42. Live — #1420, Tuesday, midnight — Jonas

Jonas tells Calloway he will go on camera with the whole map, Kessler's evidence, and Crane's name, at six the next morning, before the open, so that Strand will have no choice. Calloway tells him he will be arrested within the hour afterwards. Jonas says yes.

- **Purpose:** the decision that launches act III.
