# Chapter 49: The open

Wednesday, #1421. The rest of the day, written tonight. At 8.25 I wrote the cell and the release in the back of a car, on the reverse of a bail form, and I wrote nothing else until this evening. This is ten o'clock, the thing the other 1,420 entries were for, and I am putting it down in the order it happened, with the times, because I trust no other way of putting it down.

I had been in the room thirteen minutes. It is Walbrook's risk room, where I worked for five years, and nobody looked at me when I came through the door with blood on my shirt, which I took as a kindness. The wall screens were as they always are, the call sheet, the fails list, the balances. Only the lines were not going green. Richard Haldane stood at the back with his arms folded, the colour of a candle, and as I passed he said 'Jonas' in a voice with nothing in it, and I said 'Richard', and that was the whole of our conversation. In the corner of the centre screen was the price of insurance on a code that was not one of my twelve. It had started to move at 9.46. It was a little higher than when I had come in.

A desk had been cleared for me, with a telephone on it and a microphone on a stalk. Afterwards people will say it was a button. Walbrook had cut its data and file links at six on the Bank's advice, and the auction schedule loaded at seven could not be reloaded. It could be keyed. At the next desk sat two of Walbrook's collateral staff, a young man and a woman who looked as if she had not been home, with the schedule open in a window, and beside them the Bank of England's officer, in a headset, with the Deputy Governor's letter flat under his hand. The letter said that he might change the schedule on the instruction of Mr J. Whitlock, present in the room, and on no one else's. At 9.49 I said the new sequence aloud, thirteen codes in about forty seconds, and the woman typed it a line at a time, and the officer read each line back to me before she pressed anything. It took until 9.58. The voice line ran from my microphone to St James's and nowhere else: the fund's sterling-book head, who had put my map into her machine on Sunday, with Nasser standing behind her, and her dealers, who would buy through the three banks and needed to hear the order when the keyers did, not after. On a speaker at my elbow was the hospital. Priya, in a side room, with her wrist in plaster and Graham's sixteen box files open round her on the bed. I could hear paper.

On the centre screen, the Deputy Governor, on video, in front of her bookshelf. 'Mr Whitlock. You are the person who has the sequence.'

'Yes.'

'We shall need it without ambiguity.'

I laid the page on the desk face down. It has Walbrook's September figures on it, and one corner brown with Priya's blood. I did not need it. I turned it over anyway, so that I would not be tempted to look.

***

At ten the exchanges opened, two hours late, on a Bank statement that used the word *orderly* three times. In the first minute the thirty-year, the 2056, went from 71.50 to 70.50. A sound in the room, not a word. It was the shape I wrote down on a Tuesday when I was 36, bonds failing to arrive, except that now it was every line at once and the whole of London on one side of it.

'First,' I said.

There is no thrill in saying it. It comes out as flat as a timetable. 'Buy the 2056. What is left of the first member's collateral. Eleven hundred million. At the market.'

The officer said it into the line, a second behind me. The woman at the next desk keyed the line. Eleven hundred million pounds of government bonds, spoken into a telephone in the City of London at 10.01.

Eleven seconds. 'Done,' said the woman in St James's. 'Eleven hundred, average 70.40.' And on the screen the 2056 stopped. Not rose. Stopped, like a man on a stair who has found a step that was not there. 70.40. 70.40. 70.45.

'Second,' I said. 'The ninth member. The 2034, three hundred.'

'Stop,' said Priya. 'Thirty-five. The ninth is the 2035. The 2034 is the twelfth's.'

I had said the wrong year and the officer had it halfway to the line. He stopped it. 'Say again,' he said, and the woman at the keys took her hands off them. A minute, I think, from that to the line being clean, and I felt every second of it on the back of my neck.

'The 2035. Three hundred.'

'Done.' The ninth's bonds, which the thirteenth was holding overnight, had been recalled with the purchase, and they came home. <!-- VERIFY: recall of bonds lent overnight to another member when the borrower is under pressure, and whether they could settle back the same morning; a dramatic simplification --> The eleventh went at 10.09, the 2047, two hundred and sixty, and the twelfth at 10.12, the 2034, three hundred and forty, which was the line I had called wrong, and I called it right. The fund's three banks took the bonds to the Bank of England and came back inside the hour with cash. What I saw was the numbers going out and the numbers coming back, and a line on a side screen that said BANK OF ENGLAND: RECEIVED.

By 10.20 the three had come home. The 2035, the 2047 and the 2034 had each taken a bid the size of a street and done something I had not seen on any screen in that room for twenty days. They went up. Someone at the back made a noise like a man who has held his breath since Tuesday. The thirteenth had not been touched, and nothing of anyone else's was left in it to break.

***

10.24. 'The second member. The 2038, four hundred and ten.' Done. Then the third, the 2045, six hundred, the one that had paid its call on the Bank's money and had been selling for four hours. It took the bid and moved up four pence, and I counted.

10.35. 'The fourth member. The 2052, two hundred and ninety. I think the 2052 first, then the 2041.'

'You think,' said the Deputy Governor.

I had not heard myself say it. I have said it for four years. It is in the nine books most weeks, and nobody has ever asked me to stop. Now the Deputy Governor of the Bank of England had a clock and a country's money in her hand and was waiting, politely, for me to take it out of my sentence.

'Yes,' I said. 'I think. I have never seen it. Nobody has. I have seen what it leaves behind, and the fourth member's fingerprints say the 2052 goes about ninety seconds before the 2041. That is what I have. If you wanted a man who knows, you have not got one. You have the man who counted.'

The line hissed. Then Priya, from a hospital bed, under a name on the board that is not hers: 'He's said that to me for four years. He's been wrong on a size twice and on the order never. I'd bet the lot on it.' A rustle. 'Go on, Jonas. Say it plainly.'

'The 2052 first. Two hundred and ninety.'

10.38. 'Done.' The 2052 printed 74.50, then 74.75. The Deputy Governor nodded to someone off camera and did not speak again for half an hour.

***

10.42. The woman in St James's said it, and her voice had lost its edges; she had been on the line forty-two minutes without a break. 'Mr Whitlock. One of our three banks is also a prime broker, and it should not have told us what it has told us.' A breath. 'It sent a margin call to a fund in Mayfair at 10.42. Greywether. Two point one billion, due by two o'clock. They have, I am told, less than a third of it in cash.'

I thought: this is where they will look at me.

They looked at the screens. It was not Walbrook's number and had no business on the wall; it was a call between a bank and a fund. The Deputy Governor said, 'We shall not comment on any firm.' Then, after a pause that was the first human thing she had done in an hour: 'Thank you, Mr Whitlock.'

Greywether is not a bank. It cannot borrow from the Bank of England. It had a position that had paid by the minute for twenty days, and the position had been built on a map that I wrote, the original of which, with the notebooks, was taken from me out of a hotel cloakroom on the Tuesday night, and the same map was turning it inside out in my voice, line by line. Whatever it had done in the first gilt lines at eight, which the fund's desk had seen as small pieces from six brokers, its losses were in what it had not covered: the other lines, the shares of the parents of the failing members, the pound, and the thirteenth. It could not meet the call. I knew that by 10.43 with a certainty I have felt about nothing else this month, and I did not feel what I thought I would. I felt a hand on my elbow. The officer, who had held his headset for forty-two minutes, said, 'Sir, you've got blood on your cuff,' and I said, 'It isn't mine.' It was mine. From the nose, from Cannon Street.

10.47. The fifth member. 'The 2039. Three hundred and forty.'

'No,' said Priya. 'Two hundred. Q2, page fourteen restates it. Graham initialled the cut. The three-forty was last year's.'

The woman at the keys had already typed 340. She looked at me. I said, 'Two hundred,' and she changed it, and the officer read it back, and it was another minute. I want it written that I got a size wrong in the one hour of my life when it counted, and that a woman with a broken wrist caught it from a bed.

'Thank you,' I said.

'Thank me later.'

By 11.15 the sixth, the seventh, the eighth and the tenth had gone, each in its place, each read back, each answered, *Done*. The screens had a different colour on them. The red goes. The fails list, forty lines at ten, was nineteen, then eleven, then six. The call sheet went amber, then the pale grey that means *settled, awaiting confirmation*, then the first green, at 10.27, on the first member. And what Crane had sold and not bought back, because my map told him it would fall, the shares of the parents of the failing members, the pound, the lines beyond the first eight, was rising, in step, because each of them had a buyer under it that did not go away, and a buyer who does not go away is the only thing that has ever turned a price. A price that turns is a debt somebody has to pay.

***

11.20. The last line. By then the room had stopped looking at me and started looking at the clock, which is how I knew it was working.

'Thirteenth,' I said. 'The code Priya gave me. Three hundred and eighty million, the 2049. At the market.' And then, because there was nothing left of the sentence to hang it on: 'I think the whole of it.'

It was said, and keyed. For one second, nothing, the whole room hearing a woman in St James's not answering. Then, 'Done. Three-eighty, average 80.50.' And the thirteenth, which a man in Mayfair had been quietly building a position against for longer than anyone had had a map, which could have broken the chain at the end of the line, found a price already turned and a buyer already there, and did nothing at all. It held. There is no drama in a line holding. It is a number that stays where it is.

At 11.52 the last line on the call sheet went green. Eight minutes before the new noon. The clearing house met its deadline with the Bank's money gone round the building twice and the fund's proceeds on top. Haldane did not clap. Nobody did. A man by the window began to cry quietly, and no one looked at him.

'Mr Whitlock,' said the Deputy Governor. 'Is it over?'

Every one of the times in four years I have written *I think*, I felt it in my throat like a coin.

'Yes,' I said.

Then I wrote it on the back of the page, on the brown corner, before I had said anything else. It is the first line in 1,421 entries I have written without a hedge. I will see in the morning whether I was entitled to it.
