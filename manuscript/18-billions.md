# Chapter 18: Billions

The word had been said to Sebastian Crane eleven times before noon and he had let it stand each time.

Investors said it. That was the odd thing. Men who had spent the summer ringing to ask, with the courtesy of people who had signed a lock-up, whether the fund's position was perhaps a little large, now rang to say it as if it were a place they had visited. 'Billions, Sebastian.' A pension consultant in Edinburgh said it twice in one sentence. A family office in Geneva said it in French and then again in English, in case the first had not carried. He thanked each of them. He said that the market had done the work and that Greywether had merely declined to be surprised, which was true and which they took, as he intended, for modesty.

He did not look at the number. It was on the floor below, on 31 screens, and it would be on a sheet of paper on his desk at six when the day's prices were in, and he would read it then, once. A number looked at all day stopped meaning anything by lunchtime, the way a word repeated enough times became a noise.

What he knew without the sheet was this. The pound had opened lower than it had closed and had not found a floor. British government bonds had opened lower again, after a night in which the Asian desks had sold them for the first time as a matter of policy rather than opinion. A clearing member of the first rank was in default and its book was being hedged by a clearing house that had told the world yesterday morning, over its chief executive's signature, that its arrangements were fully sound.

Greywether was short all of it and had been since the spring. There was nothing to do but not spoil it.

At half past eleven his assistant put her head round the door to say that Sir Anthony Pell's car would be at the club at one and that Sir Anthony had asked whether Mr Crane might prefer the small dining room. Crane said the small dining room would be delightful. He had never in 30 years known Anthony Pell to ask for the small dining room when he had good news.

***

The club was in St James's and had the virtue of being the sort of place where two men could eat lunch unobserved by anyone who would admit to observing them. Pell had the corner table. He had aged, Crane thought, since Friday, in the way that men on boards aged: not in the face but in the hands, which did not know what to do with the cutlery.

'You'll have seen the day,' Pell said, which was how he had begun on Friday, and Crane let him have the small comfort of a routine.

'I'm told it continues eventful.'

'Richard was in with the Bank until two this morning.' Pell lowered his voice, though there was nobody within five tables. 'The member's margin is gone and the default fund is next. There's a second call going out intraday every time the price moves. He is, between ourselves, not entirely sure that there won't be another one by Friday.'

'Another call?'

'Another member.'

Crane said nothing. The waiter came and he ordered the sole, and Pell ordered what he always ordered and then did not touch the bread.

'That isn't why I asked you to lunch,' Pell said.

'No.'

'The man. Whitlock. You'll have seen "theft" in every paper. That's us. That's Richard's lawyers, on Sunday night, in front of a judge on the telephone, and I'm told the word did not pass the judge's lips at any point. What we have is an injunction and an order for delivery-up. Documents containing confidential information. Served on the house in Herne Hill yesterday morning. Nobody was in.' <!-- VERIFY: interim injunction and delivery-up order obtained by telephone from the out-of-hours judge on a Sunday; research/information-security.md says this is Walbrook's realistic weapon -->

'I imagine not.'

'That's the firm. Then there's the other thing, and I want you to hear it from me and not from your lunch tomorrow, because it will be lunch tomorrow.' Pell turned his knife over. 'The Treasury have gone to the police themselves.'

Crane waited.

'Carrow wrote on Sunday. Not Richard. Richard made a complaint about an access breach on Friday afternoon, a girl on Whitlock's team who logged in at his desk and pulled a report after his access had been cut; that's all the firm did, and it did it because the lawyers said it must. It was Carrow's letter that had two officers at her door on a Sunday. They held her overnight. Computer misuse, the custody sheet says. Released on bail yesterday morning, no charge. They've kept her phone and her computer and told her not to speak to anyone at the firm.' <!-- VERIFY: a Treasury director's written request leading the Met to act within hours on a Sunday; research says realistic in kind (the Damian Green pattern) but notes it is unconfirmed whether an official could in practice steer a detention -->

'The girl, Anthony?'

'Raman. Priya Raman. Thirty-one, I think. Mathematics.' Pell said it as though the degree were a condition. 'And the police want him. Whitlock. Not for the firm's benefit. For theirs. There is a woman at the Treasury who has to say the word "sound" on television every morning until polling day, and she would prefer that the man who wrote the opposite three years ago were in a room with a solicitor when she says it.'

'Caroline,' Crane said, 'has always done the right thing for the wrong reason. It is her gift.' He ate a mouthful of sole. 'And you're telling me this because?'

Pell looked at him for a long moment, and in it Crane saw the whole of Pell's weekend: the board calls, the lawyers, the gradual understanding that a man who had done nothing for four years had been keeping a note of it, in ink, with dates.

'Because if the police find him first,' Pell said, 'those books go into an evidence bag and then into a courtroom, and every page of them has Richard's name on it and Pryce's and, by the by, the dates of four board meetings I sat in. I would rather they were ash. I'm not asking what you're doing. I'd rather they were ash, Sebastian, than in anybody's hands at all. Including yours.'

'You've never wanted to know how, Anthony.'

'No.' Pell put his knife down. 'And I don't now. I'm telling you that there's a clock on it that isn't yours.'

Crane finished the sole. He thought about Anthony Pell, who had never once in 30 years said anything quickly, saying all of that in under four minutes. He thought about the word *ash*. He had no intention of burning anything. A map was not a thing one burned. One used it, and then one framed it.

'Go back to your board,' he said. 'Tell them the position is being managed. You're good at that.'

***

The sheet was on his desk at six. He read it once, folded it, and put it in the inside pocket of his jacket, which was where he had kept the first one, on another evening, in another crisis, when the number had been a tenth the size and had made his name. He did not compare them. He was not a sentimental man. He noted that the number on the sheet had moved a comma since Friday and that nobody alive could now call it luck, and he put it away.

The telephone in the drawer rang at 7.10.

'Mr Crane.'

'Dana. You have something.'

'Three things. I'll give them to you in the order I'd want them.' There was traffic behind her voice, a long straight road, a lorry. She was driving. 'One. The subject has been located. He was in a café in Clerkenwell at twenty to two this afternoon, for 50 minutes, with a man called Rhys Calloway, who writes about money for one of the nationals. He had the rucksack with him. He did not hand it over. He left on foot and my person lost him in the market, which I'm not pleased about and which he will have noticed, because he looked at her twice.'

'A journalist.'

'A journalist. The analyst's kitchen table had his name written down the margin of a printout. My person saw it on Sunday, after the police had taken her and before her colleague arrived. I've had someone on Calloway since. It's cheaper to watch the door a man has to walk through than to look for the man.' <!-- VERIFY: continuity with ch. 11 and 12. 'So you knew, on Sunday, where the girl was.'

'I knew she'd gone in a police car. I told you I'd tell you when there was something. A police car isn't something, Mr Crane. It's the absence of something.'

He let that pass. 'Two.'

'The notebooks have not been recovered. He has nine with him, in the rucksack, on his back, at all times, and he has stopped using his phone. He's bought another, cheap, for cash, or he will have; everyone does by the second day. He'll be sleeping somewhere that takes cash. I'll have the hotel by tomorrow night, but I'd like you to understand that a hotel is a public place with a hundred doors, and taking a bag off a man's back in one of them is the kind of thing that gets noticed. I don't do noticed.'

'Three.'

A pause in which the lorry passed.

'His wife and child are in Norfolk. The wife's mother is a widow in a village near Norwich; her husband's probate is on the same register as the father's garage, and it names her and the house. I drove up this morning. The wife's car was on the drive. A child's seat in the back. I didn't stop.'

Crane looked at the wall where a screen would have been. It was a quarter past seven. Below him the floor would be thinning out, the day's prices in, the number in his pocket. He thought of a man on his back on a bench somewhere with four years of other people's mistakes strapped to him, and of what that man had thought worth running from, and what he had left behind on a drive in Norfolk.

'Then you know where to apply pressure,' he said.

The line was quiet long enough that he thought the road had taken her signal.

'I don't work that way with children, Mr Crane.'

He had heard a great many people tell him what they would not do, and almost all of them had been describing a price. He listened for the price in this and did not hear one.

'I hadn't realised you had a way,' he said.

'You've never asked.' Her voice had not changed at all; it was the same voice that had told him on Friday that she had someone outside a building within the hour. 'I'll have the hotel by tomorrow. I'll have the books by the weekend, by my route. If you want a different route, you'll want a different firm, and I'd advise you to think about what a different firm looks like when it's finished.'

'Dana—'

'Goodnight, Mr Crane.'

He put the telephone back in the drawer and did not close it. He sat with his hands flat on the empty desk, as he had sat on Friday, and found that the thing he was turning over was not the hotel, or the journalist, or the number in his pocket, but a woman declining to say something on a motorway.

He had not got where he was by employing people with rules. He had got there by knowing what rules cost, and he had never yet met one that cost more than he had. But he had heard, in that silence, that this one was not for sale, and he found that he minded it less as an obstacle than as a fact about her that he had not known for as long as he had been paying her. He did not like not knowing things about people he paid.

He looked down at the drawer, at the phone with one number in it, and thought, for the first time since he had bought it, that one number was not enough.
