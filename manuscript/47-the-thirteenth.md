# Chapter 47: The thirteenth

Still #1421. I am writing the day in pieces, tonight, and this is the piece I would leave out if I were being kind to myself. I am not going to leave it out. The map I spent four years on had a hole in it, and a woman with one working hand found the hole by telephone at 8.40 in the morning, and if she had found it at 8.50 I would not be writing anything.

It was 8.38. I had been in the room two minutes and in the chair for one. Forty desks, nine of them occupied, the dark walls, and the clock that someone had made on a computer: 10:00, and under it 12:00. Nasser at the head of the table. The sterling-book head, whom I will call by her title because she has not told me her name and I have not asked, with her pen over the sixth line; her legal man with the one-page indemnity in front of him; on the wall the Deputy Governor, a woman in a blue jacket with a bookshelf behind her. We had built the first lines. I had said *I think* over one of them and been written down as a figure. And on the saucer at my elbow Priya had just said that she needed ninety seconds and that nobody was to speak.

Nobody spoke. I had not known how long ninety seconds is in a room with a clock in it.

From the speaker: a page turned. A pen cap. A trolley in a corridor and somebody saying *Raman* again, further off. The legal man began to tap the table with one finger and stopped when Nasser looked at him. The Deputy Governor lifted her own pen and held it above the pad. I counted, because it is what I do. At forty I knew she was not checking a figure, because a figure takes ten seconds. At sixty I knew she had found something and was checking whether she could stand it. At seventy-five, the sterling-book head put her pen down on the desk with great care, as one puts down a glass in a church.

At 8.40 the saucer said, 'Thirteen.'

'I'm sorry?'

'I've got thirteen. Appendix C, the concentration table, by member code.' A page, loud. 'It's in the first year's table and then it isn't, for three years, except as a line in the lending schedule at the back of Q2. Four letters. Not one of your twelve. Graham's initials beside it and a word. "Remote." It was big, so it went in, and he rated it sound, which it was, and it dropped off the next table because nobody puts a sound member in a table of problems. The ninth lent its bonds to it, and the eleventh, and the twelfth. Their bonds sit with it overnight. It's the cash behind their cash.'

I did not say anything. I could feel the shape of it before I could see it, which has happened to me perhaps four times in my life and each time I have wished it had not.

'Whoever has the dealers,' she said, to the table, not me. 'Can you look at the price of insurance on that code? What it costs to protect against its failing.'

The sterling-book head looked at a screen. 'Five weeks,' she said. 'Quiet. Up eleven basis points, a bit every day. Small pieces, three brokers, never the same size twice.' She looked up. 'I assumed it was a hedge somebody was running on the system. I have seen that footprint. I have seen it on the twelve.' She said the name of the firm.

'It's not a hedge,' said Priya. 'It's a short. They've been building it for five weeks, so that at ten, when we start buying, they can start pushing. Not on the twelve. On the thirteenth.'

I said, because someone had to: 'My record doesn't have it.'

'No.'

'Because it never failed.' I heard my own voice go flat. 'I wrote down what failed. Every entry is a fingerprint of something going wrong. A sound member leaves none. I was looking at the thing that was breaking, and he was looking at what it was resting on.'

The Deputy Governor said, 'Mr Whitlock. Please say it in a sentence.'

I said it in two. The fund buys the first eleven in order, and they are fine, and by the time it reaches the twelfth the thirteenth has been under attack for an hour, and a bank that is under attack is calling in what it lent. The twelfth's bonds are at the thirteenth. They do not come home. We buy the twelfth's debt and the bonds that should stand behind it are locked in a firm that is itself going. The chain breaks at the end, where it is longest, and we have spent the Bank's money getting there.

'And the fix?' she said.

It was Priya who gave it. 'Take the ones with bonds sitting at the thirteenth, the ninth, the eleventh, the twelfth, and move them to the front. Get their bonds home first. By the time it comes to the thirteenth, the thirteenth has nothing of anyone else's to break, and you buy it last. Last, not first. If you do it first you show your hand.'

'Out of order,' said the legal man, quietly, as a man says the name of a disease.

***

I will put down the argument as it was, because I have not stopped hearing it.

The Deputy Governor said it could not be done in the time. She said it in a courteous voice and then said it again with the reasons. The one-page indemnity, signed at 7.02, covers by reference the Bank's lending sheet, which is the line-by-line list of what the Bank will lend against and in what order; a change to the order is a change to the sheet, and a change to the sheet meant the Treasury again. The order was in the three banks' instructions, three sets in three systems. And it was in Walbrook's own schedule, the list the auction would run from, which had been loaded at seven. 'Which cannot be reloaded,' she said. 'We cut Walbrook's data and file links at six, on advice, because there are people with logins on that board whom we have reason to think are talking to people we do not wish them to talk to. The feeds, the auction schedule and the collateral substitution interface are all dark. The telephones are not, and nor is settlement through the three banks. The schedule runs as loaded. I would put the minimum for any other course at an hour and forty minutes, and it is ten to nine.'

Nasser said, 'Mr Whitlock?'

It was the only thing he had said since I sat down. I said what I had been about to say since Priya said *thirteen*. That a file we could not reload could still be keyed. That the schedule was a list, and Walbrook's collateral staff could re-enter it by hand, line by line, in the risk room, with the Bank's officer beside them, if somebody told them the new order aloud. That the order was in my head and I could say it in about forty seconds, and could not write it in a form that three banks and the Treasury would accept in an hour and forty minutes. And that a voice line from the risk room to this room would let the fund's dealers, who execute through the three banks, hear it at the same moment as the people keying.

'You,' said the Deputy Governor. 'In the room. Not here.'

'There is no one else who knows the sequence or the pace.'

'You are a man on bail,' said the legal man.

'The Bank has asked that I be available to assist it.'

'Mr Whitlock is on my authority,' said the Deputy Governor. A pause. 'He is not on the Treasury's. I will ring the Chancellor's private office, and I will give you a letter, now, in my own name. It says my officer may change the schedule at Walbrook only on the instruction of Mr Whitlock, by name, present in the room. For the sequence only. Not for prices.'

'Prices are the fund's.'

'Quite.'

Priya said, from the speaker, small and level, 'He'll be wrong about something.'

'Yes,' I said.

'He'll say it as if he isn't.'

'No,' I said. 'I'll say *I think*.' I looked at the table. 'It's the only way I know how to be right about this. If I am sure, I stop checking.'

Nobody said anything. The Deputy Governor almost smiled, then looked at her watch.

'You will give the order to my people at Walbrook by ten,' she said. 'Not after. At ten the exchanges open and the first line will be offered whether or not it is the right one. The letter will be with my officer before you are.'

Nasser had already put the telephone into his pocket. 'The car is at the door, Mr Whitlock. It will be forty minutes if the Strand is open. Mr Al-Mansouri will want you to go.'

'Does he know?'

'I told him as you spoke,' said Nasser. 'He said you are to go. He said also that I am to tell you he has never been given an instruction by a man who said *I think*, and that he finds he does not mind it.'

It was 9.04. There is a line in the third notebook, at the end of a week of fails that I could not make fit: *the thing I cannot see is the thing that is holding the rest up.* I wrote it more than two years ago. The book is in a steel cabinet in Mayfair and I did not need it. I said the line to myself in the lift, on the way down, as one reads a letter from a stranger.

The car had a driver and nothing else. He did not ask where.
