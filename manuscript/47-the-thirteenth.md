# Chapter 47: The thirteenth

Still #1421. I am writing the day in pieces, in the evening, and this is the piece I would leave out if I were being kind to myself. I am not going to leave it out. The map I spent four years on had a hole in it, and a woman with one working hand found the hole by telephone at 8.40 in the morning, and if she had found it at 8.50 I would not be writing anything.

The room in St James's was long and low and had no windows on the street side, only screens, fourteen of them, and nothing on any screen but the grey bar that says *pre-open*. Eleven people at desks, all on the telephone. At the head, at a table that had been pulled out of line for the purpose, the fund's sterling-book head, whom I will call by her title because she has not told me her name and I have not asked; her legal man at her elbow with the indemnity in front of him, one page, signed in the Treasury's hand at 7.02; Nasser standing, since he does not sit in other people's rooms; and on the wall a face. The Governor's deputy, on the Bank's video link, in a room that looked like every other room.

We had been going over the sequence for twenty-five minutes. Twelve lines. For each, which bonds the member would be forced to sell, in what quantity, at what price the fund would bid, through which of the three banks. They had my page on the table, the rebuilt one with the brown corner, and a second copy on the wall behind the deputy. I read it aloud, row by row, and they checked each row against what the fund's dealers had already keyed. It was good work. It was the work I have wanted, for four years, to watch people do.

On the table a loudspeaker telephone, and on the loudspeaker, since eight, Priya.

She was in the side room, with the plaster, with Pryce's sixteen box files in two stacks on the bed, and she had been going through Q2, which was the last one he signed, a page at a time while we spoke, because she said it was quicker than listening to us. For thirty-five minutes the speaker had made small sounds: a page turned, a pen cap. At 8.38, in the middle of me saying the ninth, there was none.

'Stop,' she said.

I stopped.

'Jonas, where's Greywether in the appendix?'

'It isn't. It's a counterparty, not a member. Greywether trades through members. It's in the envelope.'

'Not Greywether. What it's *standing on*.' A page, loud. 'Appendix C. The concentration table, by member code. How many lines do you have in it?'

'Twelve.'

'I've got thirteen.'

Nobody in the room moved. The sterling-book head stopped writing.

'It's in the first year's table,' said Priya, 'and then it isn't, for three years, except as a line in the lending schedule at the back of Q2. Four letters. Not one of your twelve. Graham's initials beside it and a word. "Remote." It was big, so it went in, and he rated it sound, which it was, and it dropped off the next table because nobody puts a sound member in a table of problems. The ninth lent its bonds to it, and the eleventh, and the twelfth. Their bonds sit with it overnight. It's the cash behind their cash.'

I did not say anything. I could feel the shape of it before I could see it, which has happened to me perhaps four times in my life and each time I have wished it had not.

'Whoever has the dealers,' she said, to the table, not me. 'Can you look at the price of insurance on that code? What it costs to protect against its failing.'

The sterling-book head looked at a screen. 'Five weeks,' she said. 'Quiet. Up eleven basis points, a bit every day. Small pieces, three brokers, never the same size twice.' She looked up. 'I assumed it was a hedge somebody was running on the system. I have seen that footprint. I have seen it on the twelve.' She said the name of the firm.

'It's not a hedge,' said Priya. 'It's a short. They've been building it for five weeks, so that at ten, when we start buying, they can start pushing. Not on the twelve. On the thirteenth.'

I said, because someone had to: 'My record doesn't have it.'

'No.'

'Because it never failed.' I heard my own voice go flat. 'I wrote down what failed. Every entry is a fingerprint of something going wrong. A sound member leaves none. I was looking at the thing that was breaking, and he was looking at what it was resting on.'

The deputy, from the wall: 'Mr Whitlock. Please say it in a sentence.'

I said it in two. The fund buys the first eleven in order, and they are fine, and by the time it reaches the twelfth the thirteenth has been under attack for an hour, and a bank that is under attack is calling in what it lent. The twelfth's bonds are at the thirteenth. They do not come home. We buy the twelfth's debt and the bonds that should stand behind it are locked in a firm that is itself going. The chain breaks at the end, where it is longest, and we have spent the Bank's money getting there.

'And the fix?' said the deputy.

It was Priya who gave it. 'Take the ones with bonds sitting at the thirteenth, the ninth, the eleventh, the twelfth, and move them to the front. Get their bonds home first. By the time it comes to the thirteenth, the thirteenth has nothing of anyone else's to break, and you buy it last. Last, not first. If you do it first you show your hand.'

'Out of order,' said the legal man, quietly, as a man says the name of a disease.

I will put down the argument as it was, because I have not stopped hearing it.

The deputy said it could not be done in the time. He said it in a courteous voice and then said it again with the reasons. The sequence was in the indemnity, which referred to the schedule, which the Treasury had initialled at 7.02 line by line; a change meant the Treasury again. The sequence was in the three banks' instructions, three sets, in three systems; a change meant three sets. And it was in Walbrook's own default schedule, the list the auction would run from, which had been loaded at seven. 'Which cannot be reloaded,' he said, 'from here. We cut Walbrook's external connections at six, on advice, because there are people with logins on that board whom we have reason to think are talking to people we do not wish them to talk to. The schedule runs as loaded. I'd put the minimum for any other course at an hour and forty minutes, and it is a quarter to nine.'

Nasser said, 'Mr Whitlock?'

It was the only thing he had said in half an hour. I said what I had been about to say since Priya said *thirteen*. That the schedule was a list, and a list is a file, and the only thing that stopped us changing the file was that nobody could reach the keyboard. That the people at the keyboard in the risk room were Walbrook's. That they would do what the Bank's people in the risk room told them. And that the Bank's people in the risk room would do what the Bank in this room told them, once, if somebody who knew the new order said it aloud, in the room, without a line to cut.

'You,' said the deputy.

'The order is in my head. I rebuilt it on a pad last Wednesday and again on Friday, on a page, with Graham's figures. I did not write down the thirteenth because I did not know it. Priya has now told me. I can say the new sequence in about forty seconds. I cannot write it down in a form that three banks and the Treasury will accept in an hour and forty minutes. If I am standing in front of the desk, I don't need to.'

'You are a man on bail,' said the legal man.

'The Bank has asked that I be available to assist it.'

'Mr Whitlock is on my authority,' said the deputy. A pause. 'He is not on the Treasury's. The Treasury has signed a schedule. I will ring the Chancellor's private office, and I will give you a letter, now, in my own name, that says my people at the desk may act on your word. For the sequence only. Not for prices.'

'Prices are the fund's.'

'Quite.'

Priya said, from the speaker, small and level, 'He'll be wrong about something.'

'Yes,' I said.

'He'll say it as if he isn't.'

'No,' I said. 'I'll say *I think*.' I looked at the table. 'It's the only way I know how to be right about this. If I am sure, I stop checking.'

Nobody said anything. The deputy, from the wall, almost smiled, then looked at his watch.

'You will give the order to my people at Walbrook by ten,' he said. 'Not after. At ten the exchanges open and the first line will be offered whether or not it is the right one.'

Nasser had already put the telephone into his pocket. 'The car is at the door, Mr Whitlock. It will be forty minutes if the Strand is open. Mr Al-Mansouri will want you to go.'

'Does he know?'

'I told him as you spoke,' said Nasser. 'He said you are to go. He said also that I am to tell you he has never been given an instruction by a man who said *I think*, and that he finds he does not mind it.'

It was 9.04. There is a line in the third notebook, at the end of a week of fails that I could not make fit: *the thing I cannot see is the thing that is holding the rest up.* I wrote it more than two years ago. The book is in a steel cabinet in Mayfair and I did not need it. I said the line to myself in the lift, on the way down, as one reads a letter from a stranger.

The car had a driver and nothing else. He did not ask where.
