# Chapter 7: Not yet

#1402

Second sitting. Past eleven. Laura has gone up. The people now.

After Haldane's handshake I went back to my desk and I did what I should have done at nine. I pulled the morning apart.

Priya had already started. She had not been asked. She had the forty calls in a sheet with three columns she had added herself: how paid, cash or bonds; which bonds; and whether we had seen those bonds anywhere else in the last five working days. The third column was the one that mattered and she had filled it in by hand from Thursday's failed check and from memory, because the system does not keep that column. The system does not think anyone would want it. <!-- VERIFY: whether a CCP's systems would track re-use of a specific ISIN across members over time; the chapter assumes not -->

She turned her screen towards me without a word. Seven lines in the third column said *yes*.

Seven. Out of forty. Not two.

'Thursday was two,' I said.

'Thursday was one fund and one check. This is everyone, on a bad day, in a hurry.' She said it flat, the number first, as she does. 'Seven members paid this morning's call, in part, with bonds that were already standing behind something else when the week began. If Monday is worse than today, and Monday is going to be worse than today, those seven have to find the bond or find the cash, and they do not have the bond.'

'How much.'

She told me. I am not going to write the figure here because it is hers and because it was wrong, in the sense that it was too small; she had only counted what she could see. I wrote the figure I thought was right underneath it, on paper, and showed her, and she looked at it for a while.

'You've done this before,' she said.

'Yes.'

'Not this week.'

'No.'

She did not ask the next question. She has been not asking it since Thursday and I can feel it in the room like a draught. I said, 'Put it in a memo. One page. Numbers, no adjectives. Address it to Graham and copy me. I'm going to take it upstairs at half one.'

'He'll say not yet.'

'Then I'll have it in writing that he said it.'

***

Graham's room at 1.30. The door closed, which he never does. He had the one page in front of him and he had read it, I could tell, because the corner was turned down where he had gone back to the top. Graham reads everything twice. It is the best thing about him and it slows him down by exactly the amount that matters.

'Seven,' he said.

'Seven that we can see.'

'This is a bad day, Jonas. Bad days look like this. People pay with what they have to hand.'

'They paid with what someone else has to hand.'

He didn't like that and he let me see that he didn't like it, which for Graham is a shout. He said, 'What are you asking for.'

'Formal notification to the regulator this afternoon. Under our own obligations, not as a favour.' <!-- VERIFY: the formal route by which a UK CCP notifies its supervisor of a material risk event, and whether the Bank of England is the relevant supervisor; the chapter says "the regulator" throughout --> 'That we have identified reuse of collateral across members, that it is material, and that we expect Monday's call to be larger than today's. Our number for Monday, with the assumptions. And a request that they look across the other clearing houses for the same bonds, because we can only see our own books.'

'You want me to tell the Bank, on the Friday afternoon of the worst day since—' He stopped before he named a year. 'You want me to tell them that Walbrook's collateral is not sound.'

'I want you to tell them what we know.'

'The Chancellor said at eleven that we were met in full.'

'We were met in full. That is what I'm telling you. That's the sign.'

He looked at the page again. He turned the corner down again, though it was already down.

'Simon Carrow rang me at nine this morning,' he said, and he said it to the page. 'From the Treasury. He wanted the number for the morning call, and whether it was met, and he had it in the Chancellor's mouth within two hours. That is where we are, Jonas. That is how close they are standing. If I send this upstairs at two o'clock it is in Carrow's inbox at two fifteen and in a Cabinet briefing by four and on the front of a newspaper by Sunday with a photograph of this building, five weeks before an election, and nobody will read your assumptions.'

'Then they'll read them on Monday afternoon.'

'Not yet,' Graham said.

I waited. He has said *not yet* to me perhaps thirty times in five years and it has never once meant *later*.

'Let's see Monday,' he said. 'If Monday is what you say, we go together, with the day's figures, and nobody can say we jumped. If Monday is a bad day that passes, as they do, as they have, then we have not put Walbrook's name in a report that cannot be withdrawn.' He put his hand flat on the page. 'I'm asking you to take this offline until then.'

I said, 'Graham, you filed the last one.'

I had never said that to him. Three years and I had never said it. He went a colour I have not seen on him and he did not answer, and I took my copy of Priya's page and went out, and I did not close the door behind me because he never does.

***

At 2.10 I filed it myself.

There is a route. It exists for exactly this and nobody uses it, because using it is the end of your career at whichever firm you use it from, and everybody knows that and nobody says it. A named individual at a clearing house may notify the regulator directly of a matter he believes the firm has failed to report. <!-- VERIFY: whether UK CCP staff have a formal individual route for reporting to the supervisor, or whether this would in practice be whistleblowing under the general regime --> I had the form bookmarked. I had bookmarked it when I was 37, the week after the memo went in and nothing came back, and I had never opened it since.

Priya's page. My figure for Monday underneath, with the assumptions. Four paragraphs that I wrote in eleven minutes and did not reread. My name, my title, my direct line. The time stamp said 14.21.

I did not tell Priya. I should have. She would have put her name on it and I did not want her name on it, and I am aware that is the same reasoning Graham uses about Walbrook.

Then I went back to work, because the afternoon call was due at three and Monday does not care who I have written to. <!-- VERIFY: whether a UK CCP would run a second scheduled intraday call on a stress day, or ad hoc calls -->

***

At 3.40 the collateral database would not open.

I tried it twice. Then I tried the margin engine. Read-only. Then the member files: present, but the account-level view was gone, so I could see that a firm had paid and not what with. The three columns Priya had built were on her screen, not mine, and her access was untouched. I checked. I did not tell her why I was checking.

I rang the help desk and the help desk said that a change had been applied to my profile at 15.32 at the request of the chief risk officer's office, and would I like a ticket. I said no.

So that was nine minutes. A notification went to the regulator at 14.21, and somebody at the regulator rang somebody here, and at 15.32 my access was cut to what a graduate gets on his first morning. An hour and eleven minutes. I have never seen Walbrook do anything in an hour and eleven minutes.

At 4.00 Graham came to my desk. Not his room. Mine, in the open, where Priya could hear and where he could leave quickly. He stood beside my chair and looked at the screen, which was showing a login error, and he did not look at me. In five years he has always looked at me.

'Richard has asked,' he said, 'and I'm passing this on as it was given to me, Jonas, so take it as that. Richard has asked that any personal notes relating to Walbrook's business be made available to him. Any notes, in any form, kept anywhere. He'd like to see them on Monday morning.'

'Personal notes.'

'Those were the words.'

'Whose words.'

He did not answer that. He said, 'It's a request. For now it's a request,' and he went back to his glass box and shut the door and sat down with his back to the room, and I watched him for a while and I don't think he moved.

Priya said, very quietly, 'What notes.'

I said, 'Work,' which is what I say to Laura.

***

Here is the thing I did not understand until 4.35, sitting at a desk that no longer worked, and I want it down exactly because it is the kind of thing I will later pretend I had worked out sooner.

*Personal notes.* Not *reports*, not *analysis*, not *files*. Haldane does not know the word *notebook* and he does not know the word *record*. He knows that something exists that is personal and is in note form and relates to Walbrook's business, and he knows it well enough to ask for it by Monday, and he has known it long enough that the request was ready before my filing was an hour old. Graham was formal with me all day Thursday. Graham was in at 5.50 this morning. Haldane shook my hand at noon and said *well held* and looked at me a half-second longer than he looks at anyone.

They did not learn this today. They learned it on Wednesday.

What did I do on Wednesday.

I turned 40. I photographed Tuesday night's page at 6.41 in the morning, standing in the kitchen with Elsie on my hip, the way I have photographed every page for 1,399 mornings, and the phone chose the address for me. I wrote that down on Wednesday night, when I found it had not arrived. I wrote that someone had opened it. I did not know who.

I opened my sent items at 4.37 to look at it again. My mail still works. It is the only thing that does.

Wednesday, 6.41. Subject: 1399. One attachment, one image. Addressed to my Walbrook address, not my own. Same first letter. The phone's choice, and mine for not checking.

The image is the spread. The right-hand page is #1399. The left-hand page is the end of #1398: three lines of gilts, a member's code, and a note about a line posted here and out on repo somewhere else at the same time. Graham's initials on a quarterly report. The word contained.

So they have one entry and the tail of another. The entry tells them nothing but my age and a few years counted off. The tail tells them what the books are for. And the two together tell them the thing that matters: that there are 1,398 others, and that someone has been counting.

That is what Graham's four words on Wednesday were. That is what the request at four o'clock was. I knew on Wednesday night that it had been opened. I know tonight who opened it, and where it went after that.

That is why I went to Lewisham tonight. That is why the old books stay there.

And one more thing, which I did not see until I had written the rest down. Carrow had our morning number by nine. The Chancellor had it by eleven. My filing went in at 14.21 and my access was gone by 15.32, and Haldane's request for my notes was ready and worded and in Graham's mouth by four. Walbrook does not move at that speed. Nobody in this building does.

*Whose words*, I asked Graham, and he did not answer. I think he did not answer because the answer is not Richard Haldane.
