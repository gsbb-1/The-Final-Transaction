# Chapter 37: No

He had asked for no officials, and Caroline Strand had said yes to that on the telephone at half past twelve and then, at half past seven, brought two. Simon Carrow, at her left, where he had been for three years, and the young man with the laptop at the end of the table, who had typed through the Saturday meeting a fortnight ago and would type through this one. A week ago she had given the Governor a room with no note in it and had found that it did not help. She was not going to make the same mistake with a foreign state.

The same room. She had chosen it. Third floor, the long table, the park beyond the glass gone from grey to black while she waited, nothing on the walls. Sunday, eight o'clock. The building was Sunday-empty and the lights in the corridor had come on for them in sections.

Al-Mansouri came in first, which she noted. A good dark suit, no tie, a man who had been tired for a long time and had decided not to mention it; he shook her hand and the Governor's and sat where she put him, and did not look round the room, because he had been in rooms. His chief of staff stayed outside with the door. Then the Governor, who had come on foot again, in the same coat, and who had telephoned her at one o'clock to say that he would attend and that she should understand what his attending meant. She understood it. He sat on her right, which was his place, and did not take out a pen.

Then Whitlock.

He had been searched downstairs, courteously, by her own people, on her instruction, and had carried in nothing but a coat that had been torn at the knee and mended by someone who did not sew. He stood for a moment inside the door and looked at the table, and she watched him find the chair he had sat in two weeks ago, at the far end, and take a different one, nearer, and sit down in it, and put his hands flat on the wood where she could see them. He had learned that somewhere. She wondered from whom.

There were four police officers in London who would have liked very much to know that this man was in this building. She had thought about that in the car and decided it in the car. A man wanted for questioning, arrested in the Treasury, on a Sunday night, having arrived as the guest of a sovereign wealth fund: that was Monday's front page, and it was not a page about collateral. She had said to the Governor, a fortnight ago, that a story about a man was easier to run than a story about collateral. It was still true. It was now true against her.

'Mr Al-Mansouri. You asked for thirty minutes. The Governor has told me what you are going to ask for. I would like to hear it from you, in your words, and then I will ask Mr Whitlock some questions, and then I will tell you my decision tonight, because I understand that time is the point.'

'It is the whole of the point, Chancellor.' Formal, unhurried, the English of a man who had learned it young and kept it carefully. 'I will be brief, because the Governor has been thorough.'

He was brief. On Tuesday morning the fund he directed would buy, through its London clearing banks, the government bonds that twelve named members of Walbrook Clearing House would be forced to sell to meet their calls, in the order and in the quantities set out on a page that Mr Whitlock had, in the sense that he could produce it, and had, in the sense that he could not. The Bank of England would lend to the fund's banks against the bonds as they were bought, so that the fund's cash went round rather than out. The Bank and the exchanges would open late, and Walbrook would move its deadline to noon. The Treasury would indemnify the Bank against loss. If it worked, and he said "if" without emphasis, the forced selling would stop by nine, the second member would survive the morning, and anyone who had sold those twelve names short would need to buy them back from a market in which there was one buyer, who was not them. If it did not, his fund would have returned to London the greater part of what it had spent a week removing, and would lose it, and the clearing house would fail in any case, and he would not be the man answering for it at home, because he would not be at home.

He had not raised his voice or dropped it. He had, she noticed, not once said the word "state".

'Governor.'

'It is a repo, Chancellor. It is what the Bank does. I would do it tomorrow morning if you would sign the indemnity, and I would have the exchanges and Walbrook on the telephone tonight. I have said as much to you for a week. The difference tonight is that there is a buyer. Last Saturday there was not.'

'And the scale.'

He gave it. She had it already, from the one o'clock call, and had spent the afternoon with it, and hearing it aloud in the room did not improve it.

She turned to Whitlock. She had crossed better witnesses, and worse, and she knew within a minute which this was: the kind who answered the question asked and no other, which was the hardest kind, because it left nothing to hang him with except what he said.

'Mr Whitlock. The order in which these twelve institutions fail. You cannot prove it.'

'No.'

'You have inferred it.'

'From four years of what I saw. Yes.'

'The notebooks in which you saw it.'

'Were taken from me on Tuesday night.'

'By whom?'

'I was not told. I can tell you who has them now.'

'I did not ask you that.' She let the silence sit, a barrister's habit, and he let it sit too, which almost nobody did. 'So the Treasury is asked to indemnify the Bank of England to lend against purchases made by a foreign state, in an order the state cannot verify, from a list that exists on one page in your coat and otherwise in your memory, because the evidence for it is in the possession of someone you are not prepared to name in this room.'

'The evidence for it is on Tuesday morning, Chancellor. The first name on the list failed at 7.58 on the Monday before last. I had it first. The second name is borrowing from the Bank to pay its calls. I had it second. You can wait for the third and the fourth and then you will have proof, and no clearing house.'

She looked at the Governor. The Governor looked at the table.

'Mr Al-Mansouri. Your country is, as of yesterday evening, the subject of a motion in the American Senate to freeze its assets in connection with the sinking. Your government denies it. You will understand that I have to proceed on the basis of what is said in public, not what is said in rooms.'

'My government did not sink the ship, Chancellor.'

'I note that you say so, and that you offer me nothing with it.' He inclined his head, very slightly, as if she had made a fair point in a game. 'And you are, I am told, recalled.'

'For consultations.'

'Which may not go well.'

'Which may not.'

'Then I would be signing a piece of paper that lets a state publicly blamed for this fortnight buy, at the bottom, what British pension funds are forced to sell, with the Bank lending it the money to do so and the taxpayer carrying the loss; and by Friday the man I signed it with may have been replaced by one who holds those bonds and does not care for London at all.'

'He would hold them, Chancellor,' the Governor said, 'at the Bank. Against cash. That is what a repo is. Whoever runs the fund on Friday, the bonds are in my vault, and if he does not want them back I keep them and he keeps the cash, and I have bought gilts at Tuesday's price with the Treasury's name on it, which is what you have been refusing to let me do for a week.'

'Buying British institutions.' She said it to see what it did in the room. 'That is what it will be called.'

'Bonds,' Whitlock said. 'Not institutions. Government bonds, which your department sold him in the first place.'

'And you believe the public will tell the difference.'

He did not answer that, because it was not a question, and she saw that he knew it was not.

***

She stood, because she thought better standing, and went to the glass. The park was black. Horse Guards was lit, and empty, and beyond it the Admiralty, and beyond that the city that had spent a fortnight learning the word "collateral".

The prime minister had given her until Monday to stabilise the situation. He had used that phrase, and she had let him. On Thursday a journalist had printed that her department had asked the police to detain a woman who pulled a report for her boss, and she had said that the Treasury did not comment on police matters, and the sentence had held for about a day. On Thursday night she had read the memo. All of it, the tables, the footnotes, the sentence everyone knew and the pages nobody had turned. It was right. She had sat with that for an hour and then put it back under its cover note, where it had been for three years, because a department's knowledge was the department's, and the doctrine had a name, and she had been going to need it.

What she had in front of her was a mechanism that would work. She could see that it would work. It was the Governor's own proposal from a week ago with a buyer in it, and the buyer was the one thing that had been missing, and the one thing she could not be seen beside. Two and a half weeks. Nineteen days. A Gulf state, a Treasury indemnity, a bailout with a foreign flag on it and a wanted man in the dealing room. She did not need to imagine the front page. She could have written it, and the Opposition's line under it, and the question at the first press conference, and her answer, and the next question, and she found she could not write the second answer, and that was the test, and she had known it in the car.

She had the other page too. The one for Wednesday, if Tuesday went as the Governor said. She had not let herself write that one, because she had found, a week ago, that she thought about the memo instead of the decision, and had recognised the manoeuvre, and she recognised it now. She was thinking about Wednesday's page because it was easier than Monday's.

The thing about belief, she had told this man at this table, was that it was the mechanism. She had meant the market's belief. She found she had not, until this fortnight, considered whose else.

She turned round.

'No.'

Nobody moved. The young man at the end of the table had stopped typing and she waited until he started again.

'I want to be exact, because I would like the exactness recorded. I am not refusing the Bank. The Governor may lend to whom he judges fit, against what he judges fit, on his own authority and his own balance sheet, and he knows that. What I am declining to do is to put the Treasury's name to it. The government will not indemnify a transaction in which a foreign sovereign fund, whose country is the subject of a freezing motion in a friendly legislature, buys British government debt at distressed prices in an order dictated by a man the police wish to interview and whose evidence he cannot produce. Not because of the election. Because no Chancellor could defend it to a select committee in any month of any year, and I am not going to be the first to try. That is not politics, Governor. It is prudence.'

She heard the sentence land, and heard what it was, and did not take it back.

Al-Mansouri stood. He did it without hurry and buttoned his jacket, and she saw that he had expected it, and that expecting it had not helped him either.

'Thank you for the thirty minutes, Chancellor. I shall go home. I had hoped to be able to tell them that London had asked me to stay.' He paused at the door. 'When it is useful to you to know who sank the ship, I will still know. I do not expect that to be soon.'

Whitlock got up more slowly. He had not looked at her since "bonds". He looked at her now, from the chair he had chosen, and said, quite quietly, as if he were noting a time: 'You asked me for a signature once. I asked for the night. You gave me until six on Monday.'

'I remember.'

'It's Sunday,' he said, and went out behind the man who had brought him, and she heard, through the door, the chief of staff say something in a language she did not have.

***

The Governor did not get up. He sat with his hands folded and looked at the place at the table where the fund had been.

'Walbrook's call goes to the banks at eight on Tuesday,' he said. 'The auction opens at nine. I will be lending to the second member from seven. I will keep lending until there is nothing left to lend against, which on my figures is Tuesday afternoon. After that I write to you. The letter will say what I told you in the flat it would say.'

'I know what it will say.'

'Then you know that once it exists, you may direct me to do precisely what you have just declined to indemnify, and that the only difference will be the date on it and the number of institutions still standing.' He stood, and did up the coat. 'I am not arguing, Chancellor. You have decided. I am telling you the timetable, because people always think it is midnight on Monday.'

He saw himself out.

Carrow waited until the lift had gone. He had the face he wore when he had been given something he could not make smaller, and she had hired him for it, and tonight she did not want to see it.

'Chancellor. The officers downstairs would like to know whether the gentleman who came in with the fund has left the building.'

'He came in as the guest of a foreign state. He may leave as one. The Treasury does not involve itself in operational policing.' She picked up her bag. 'And Simon. For the minutes. The word tonight was prudence. I would like it in.'

'Yes, Chancellor.'

He had already written it down. She had watched him do it, at the moment she said it, and she had watched him not look up, and she found, walking to the lift along a corridor whose lights came on in sections ahead of her and went off behind, that she could not remember whether she had rehearsed the word in the car, or only found it in the room, and that for the first time in twenty-six years at the Bar and in politics she could not tell the difference.
