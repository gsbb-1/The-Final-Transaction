# Chapter 26: Terms

He wanted it in person, which told Dana Kessler most of what she needed to know before she had sat down.

Clients who were pleased rang. Clients who were frightened wanted you in the room, where they could watch your face while they said the thing they had decided to say, and then tell themselves afterwards that you had agreed to it. She arrived at four minutes past nine, by the quiet street, past a parking warden writing a ticket with great care, and was shown up by a young man who did not ask her name because he had been told not to.

The room had no screens. She had been in it three times and had never liked it. A man who paid thirty-one people to watch prices on the floor below him and would not have one on his own wall was a man who wanted the numbers brought to him by hand, by someone else, so that they arrived already shaped. It was not modesty. It was a way of never having been told.

'Dana. Sit.'

She sat. Crane did not. He stood at the window with his hands behind his back and let her see that he had been standing there a while.

'You said Norfolk,' he said.

'Friday to Sunday. The wife's mother's house, a village outside Norwich. He arrived Friday night, late, by a route that cost him most of a day, in a hire car in the journalist's name, which my people watched leave Kentish Town with two men in it. He stayed in. The wife, the child, the mother-in-law. Nobody else came or went until the paper named him yesterday morning. At twenty to seven last night he left the house and came back to London the long way, five hours of it. One of my people stayed with him. He went to the journalist's flat in Kentish Town, where the analyst is, and the light went off at ten to one. He had the rucksack on his back the whole way, both straps, the way you carry a child's bag. The notebooks have not left his body since the Sunday before last. I would be surprised if they left it to shower.'

'And you did not take them on the train.'

'A train is a tube with witnesses, Mr Crane. Six carriages, a guard, and sixty people with phones who had seen his photograph that morning. No.'

He turned round at that, and she saw his face properly for the first time, and revised her estimate of the morning downward. He looked like a man who had slept and been congratulated and had, somewhere in the small hours, decided something.

'The price has gone up,' he said.

'Whose?'

'Yours. There is no offer to him any more. I withdrew it at half past four last Monday and I am not renewing it; a man who is on the front page is not a man I can pay. So I am no longer buying, I am acquiring, and that is a different kind of work and I will pay for it as such.' He named the figure. It was double what the retainer had been, without a breath on either side. 'And the urgency has gone up with it.'

'The paper.'

'Not the paper. The paper is a nuisance; it makes him visible, and it makes him feel safe, and both of those will be useful to you by the end of the week.' He sat down at last, on his own side of the empty desk, and put his hands flat on it. 'On Friday afternoon two of my brokers rang me within an hour of each other, which they have never done in their lives, to tell me in a roundabout way that a client of theirs had asked for the dates of certain trades of mine. A sovereign client. Not the trades themselves, which they would not have given, but the dates on which the positions were put on. Somebody in a Gulf capital has a very large loss and a very good analyst, and the analyst has noticed that I was positioned for a shipping accident before there had been one.'

'Were you?'

'I was positioned for a shock, Dana. I have been positioned for a shock since the spring, and I said so to anyone who would listen, and most of them laughed. The dates are a coincidence of the kind that a fund which has lost a great deal of money will find hard to forgive.' He smiled, briefly, the smile he used when he was pleased with a sentence. 'The point is this. The fund has the money to stop what is happening, if it knew where to put it. It does not know where. He does. If those two meet before I have the books, I am no longer the only man in London with the map, and a map that two people have is a map that nobody has. I want the notebooks by the weekend. By whatever route works.'

She let a few seconds go by, because she had learned long ago that a client would fill a silence with the thing he had been trying not to say, and because she wanted it said.

He did not fill it. He was better than most.

'The routes,' she said, 'in order. Watch. Search. Offer. Pressure. Force. We have watched, we have searched, we have offered, twice. We are at pressure, and I applied it on Friday, in a kitchen, and the result was that the subject went to his wife rather than away from her, which is a result I have seen before and did not expect from him. Force on a man with a rucksack in central London is a mugging, and a mugging goes wrong one time in three and makes the news the other two. Which leaves pressure, applied harder. Pressure, Mr Crane, means the family. You know that. You said so yourself last Tuesday.'

'I said you would know where to apply it.'

'You did. And I said how I work.'

'And I said I hadn't realised you had a way.' He said it lightly. He was enjoying himself, she saw, which was worse than if he had been angry. 'I'm not asking for a method. I have never once asked you for a method; you've said so to me, with some pride. I am asking for a result by the weekend and telling you that the route is yours to choose. Whatever works.'

'Does whatever works include the child?'

She asked it exactly as she had asked the time of the train. She had decided on the stairs that she would ask it, and that she would ask it in that voice, so that whatever he answered would be an answer and not a reaction.

He did not say no.

He looked at her for a moment with an expression she could only have described as interest, as if she had shown him a card he had not known was in the pack, and then he said, 'Dana, I have told you what I want,' and then he said nothing else at all.

She counted to five. She did not hear a no.

'I'll be in touch,' she said, 'when there is something to hear.' And she stood, and buttoned her coat, and went to the door without having agreed to anything, which she had done perhaps three times in fifteen years of government service and never since.

'By the weekend,' he said to her back.

'I heard you, Mr Crane.'

The firm had three rooms over a dry cleaner's and no name on the door, and when she came in at twenty past ten the one of her three who did not do surveillance looked up and then looked down again, which was the correct response to her face. <!-- VERIFY: the firm's premises are not in the ledger; "three rooms over a dry cleaner's", location unnamed, is new and minimal -->

She sat at her own desk and did what she did with every subject on the first morning, which was the first fifteen minutes. She told everyone who worked for her the same thing: most of what people believe is hidden is on a public register for a few pounds, and the trick is not access but order. You do the registers before you do anything else, because the registers do not know you have looked.

She opened a new file. She gave it a number, as she gave every subject a number, in the sequence, after Whitlock's.

Companies House. Greywether Capital, the limited liability partnership and the two companies that were its members; the designated members; the filing history for ten years, every change of registered office and every confirmation statement, downloaded in the order they had been filed. The FCA register, the firm's permissions, the individuals approved to run it, the dates. The Land Registry: the building on the quiet street, held through a company whose own filings she opened in a second window; the house in the country she had not known about until the title showed it; the flat. The Electoral Commission: donations by individual, by year, to a party she did not need to look up, in amounts she wrote down without comment because a figure was a figure. The Charity Commission: a museum trust, a trustee's name, the year he had joined. <!-- VERIFY: Greywether's legal structure (LLP with corporate members, feeder fund offshore) is a plausible UK hedge-fund structure and is invented here; none of the registers named would show a fund's trading positions -->

None of it was secret. All of it was Crane. That was the point of the fifteen minutes: not to find the thing, but to know the shape of the man before the thing was found, so that when it was, you would know where it fitted.

Then she stopped and did what she would have told anyone else was the second step, which was to write down the question. She wrote: *What did he know, and when.*

The registers would not tell her that. A fund's positions were not public; a short on a government's bonds and its currency left no mark on any register she could reach. But dates were different. Dates were everywhere. A tanker had sunk on a Thursday night, and that was a date. A fund had, by its own founder's admission in a room with no screens, been positioned before it. The sovereign fund's analyst, whoever he or she was, had got as far as asking brokers for dates. If a very large loss and a very good analyst could get there from the outside, so could she, and she had one thing the analyst did not, which was the client's own voice, that morning, saying *the dates are a coincidence* in the tone he used for sentences he had rehearsed.

She wrote the date of the sinking at the top of a clean page, and under it the word *before*, and left the rest of the page empty, because the rest of the page was the job.

The rule about children was not a moral position. She had said so to Whitlock in the park, about something else, and it was true of this as well. It was a rule. Rules were what you had instead of judgement at four in the morning, when judgement was the first thing to go. She had made it a long time ago and she had never had to explain it to a client, because no client had ever before failed to say no.

She did not write that down.

At 11.06 her phone went. The man she had put on the subject, from a street she could picture.

'He's gone in,' he said. 'Office building, St James's. Walked straight up to the desk. Gave his name. His real one, out loud, like he wanted it heard.'

'Whose building?'

He read her the plate beside the door.

It was the name Crane had not said.
